Coordinate Product Listings, Stock and Marketplace Orders

Thematic Area: Market Access and Business Growth

How might we manage supported marketplace listings, stock and orders through a shared process that fits the retailer’s online operations?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer and its prepared-food business use online sales channels alongside physical retail. This project focuses on centralised marketplace management, with overselling, listing accuracy and administrative effort included in the baseline assessment.

The objective is to simplify selected online activities through shared product, inventory and order information. The workflow must connect a marketplace order to stock reservation, fulfilment and a status update.

Relevant startup capabilities include multichannel commerce software, listing tools, inventory synchronisation and shipping integration. Document the company’s active Indonesian marketplace accounts and current systems, then select the functions to improve.

Significance of Problem

  • Inconsistent channel information: Separately maintained listings and availability records expose customers to outdated product or stock information.
  • Higher administrative effort: Repeated updates across channels consume staff time and make exceptions difficult to monitor.
  • Unclear fulfilment handovers: Disconnected order and stock records complicate preparation, dispatch and customer-status updates.

Existing Efforts

  • Available platform capability: A subscription multichannel platform consolidates listings, inventory, orders, shipping labels and sales reporting across supported marketplaces, including Shopee and Tokopedia. Demonstrate the required features using the retailer’s Indonesian accounts as part of platform selection.
  • First application: Connect one account or a small set of supported channels using representative products. Demonstrate stock updates, order imports, reservations, cancellations and dispatch-status handling before extending the workflow.
  • Implementation priorities: Agree the source of truth for stock, update timing, product identifiers and exception ownership. Establish food-specific fulfilment requirements and integration costs. Compare manual processing effort and record consistency with the baseline, and measure sales and delivery outcomes separately from the platform connection.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian e-commerce demand: Indonesian e-commerce GMV was projected at US$71 billion in 2025, growing by more than 14% from 2024. This figure measures merchandise value across categories and provides broader context for grocery commerce and marketplace-management tools.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
