Automate Freight Accounting and Trigger Invoicing from Delivery Evidence

Thematic Area: Logistic & Shipping

How might we automate freight accounting and use accepted proof of delivery to trigger accurate customer invoicing in the ERP?

Background

Freight-forwarding finance workflows can be improved through AI-powered accounting and better connections between proof of delivery (POD), validated shipment charges and ERP invoice issuance.

The objective is to reduce repeated data entry, improve accounting consistency and shorten the handover from completed delivery to customer billing. Once POD is accepted and agreed billing conditions are satisfied, the ERP must issue the corresponding invoice through the defined approval process.

Relevant startup capabilities include accounting automation, document processing, delivery-management software and enterprise-system integration.

Significance of the Problem

  • Slower billing: Manual checking and invoice preparation delay billing for completed deliveries.
  • More accounting errors: Repeated entry and disconnected records create incorrect charges and inconsistent financial information.
  • Higher administrative costs: Finance teams spend additional time reconciling delivery evidence, invoices and accounting records.

Potential Market Size

  • Global accounting technology market: Grand View Research estimates the accounting software and services market at US$20.8 billion in 2025, growing to US$38.7 billion by 2033. This covers the financial systems that manage invoicing, accounting and reporting across industries.
  • Invoice-to-cash automation: The global accounts receivable automation market was estimated at US$4.8 billion in 2025, with a forecast of US$12.9 billion by 2033. This includes automated invoicing, payment processing and reconciliation.
  • Indonesian freight and logistics industry: Mordor Intelligence estimates the industry at US$139.4 billion in 2026. This is the customer industry served by freight finance automation; it measures logistics services rather than software spending.

  • Lower invoice-processing costs: P&O Ferrymasters reduced invoice-processing costs by more than 35% after adopting Proactis and connecting it with Unit4 Financials. The logistics company centralised 192,000 annual invoices and accelerated proof-of-delivery document handling, showing measurable savings from connected finance workflows. (Source: Proactis customer story (opens in a new tab))
  • Faster billing from delivery evidence: VitalAire Canada reduced its order-to-invoice process from 8–10 business days to 24–48 hours after digitising proof-of-delivery documents. The system handles approximately 6,000 POD pages a month from 11 plants, showing how faster access to accepted delivery evidence shortens the billing cycle. (Source: Octacom customer story (opens in a new tab))
  • Automation already used by major logistics operators: NFI integrated Rossum with Coupa for an accounts-payable operation handling more than 150,000 invoices and credit notes annually. The workflow extracts invoice data, checks it against business records and flags exceptions, reducing manual work while preserving finance controls.
