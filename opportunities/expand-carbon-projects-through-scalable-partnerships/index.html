Expand Carbon Projects Through Scalable Partnerships

Thematic Area: Carbon Project Development and Growth

How might we identify suitable carbon projects and development partners to expand small initiatives into a commercially viable portfolio of credible emissions reductions and removals?

Background

Scaling carbon projects beyond small, standalone initiatives requires partners who can identify viable opportunities, secure access to suitable land and establish workable development arrangements.

The objective is to build a scalable project portfolio through joint operations, joint ventures or dedicated project companies. Partners could contribute project-development expertise, landowner networks, site assessment, restoration technologies and commercial feasibility analysis.

Nature-based innovation is the main focus. Other carbon-project types are welcome, with blue carbon excluded.

Significance of the Problem

  • Limited project scale: Small, separate initiatives struggle to spread development costs across a commercially viable portfolio.
  • Restricted land access: Difficulty securing suitable land and project rights prevents promising opportunities from progressing.
  • Slower investment decisions: Uncertainty over project feasibility, partner responsibilities and commercial arrangements delays investment.
  • Fragmented development: Assessing and developing each project separately increases costs and slows expansion.

Potential Market Size

  • Substantial restoration opportunity: On 6 October 2026, Indonesia’s Ministry of Forestry reaffirmed a target to rehabilitate 12 million hectares of degraded land. Its 2026–2030 National Production and Protection Forest Investment Plan also prioritises funding needs, carbon outcomes and implementable programmes. These provide context for restoration and project-development opportunities; carbon eligibility and commercial viability require assessment at each site.
  • Demonstrated carbon-finance potential: In January 2026, the Forest Carbon Partnership Facility reported that Indonesia’s East Kalimantan programme had received US$110 million for 22 million tonnes of verified CO₂-equivalent emissions reductions. This demonstrates financing for Indonesian forest-carbon outcomes, although it represents a jurisdictional programme rather than the size of the private project-development market.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.
