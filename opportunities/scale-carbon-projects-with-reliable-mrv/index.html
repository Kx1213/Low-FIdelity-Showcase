Scale Carbon Projects with Reliable MRV

Thematic Area: Carbon Project Development & Climate Technology

How might we improve measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) and expand small carbon projects through suitable partnerships to build a scalable portfolio of credible emissions reductions and removals?

Background

A shipping and logistics group wants to develop carbon projects beyond its existing small initiatives. Its projects are individually too small to scale efficiently, and limited access to underlying land creates additional investment and development challenges.

The objective is to establish reliable project-level MRV, identify suitable projects and partners, and expand through joint operations, joint ventures or dedicated project companies. Relevant startups bring capabilities in carbon project development, environmental monitoring, geospatial analysis and project documentation.

Nature-based innovation is the main focus. Other non-blue-carbon project types are also welcome; blue carbon is excluded.

Significance of Problem

Key challenges include:

  • Limited project expansion: Small initiatives face barriers to becoming a larger, viable portfolio.
  • Higher development costs: Separate monitoring and documentation processes increase the work required for individual projects.
  • Verification delays: Incomplete baseline data, monitoring records and supporting evidence slow assessment and verification.
  • Project-development barriers: Land access, project rights and partner expertise are central to feasible investment and expansion.

Existing Efforts

  • Current company approach: The group has developed an internal carbon calculator and is seeking additional project-development and MRV capabilities to support expansion.
  • Available partner capabilities: A nature-based project provider offers individual-tree tracking and a partnership supporting project development and verification documentation. A geospatial assessment provider offers land-boundary validation, supplier-evidence collection and forest-risk analysis. A secure document platform provides controlled access to project records and supporting evidence.
  • Collaboration focus: Connect project screening, monitoring and evidence preparation through a carbon-specific MRV process. Build a practical model for expanding or combining suitable projects, with independent validation and verification integrated into the project-development workflow.

Potential Market Size

  • Growing verification-services market: The global carbon-credit validation, verification and certification services market was estimated at US$366.6 million in 2025 and is forecast to reach US$2.07 billion by 2033, with 24.2% annual growth during 2026–2033. The market covers project assessment and certification services.
  • Project expansion potential in Indonesia: In October 2026, Indonesia announced a target to rehabilitate 12 million hectares of degraded land, creating a substantial land-restoration context for project assessment and monitoring.
  • Regulatory demand for verification: Indonesia’s Minister of Forestry Regulation No. 6 of 2026 requires validation and verification of forestry carbon units, including checks on baselines, additionality and achieved results. These requirements support demand for reliable project data and verification support.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
