Develop Food Products from Suitable Plant Ingredients and By-Products

Thematic Area: Food Innovation and Sustainability

How might we turn a safe, suitable plant-based ingredient or food-processing by-product into a desirable product with viable economics?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates prepared-food lines using broadly the same raw ingredients sold in its stores. This existing ingredient-to-product model provides a foundation for developing additional food products.

The objective is to identify a suitable plant ingredient or processing by-product and develop a product that creates additional value. Establish each input’s availability, condition, consistency and intended use before product development.

Relevant partner capabilities include ingredient processing, fermentation, food formulation and commercialisation support. Where the project includes surplus ingredients, the company’s quality team must set acceptance, handling and product-release requirements before their use.

Significance of Problem

  • Missed product opportunities: Suitable ingredients retain limited commercial uses without a viable formulation and customer proposition.
  • Poor processing economics: Additional handling, treatment, testing and packaging costs exceed the value recovered from a new product.
  • Inconsistent input supply: Unreliable ingredient volumes or quality prevent consistent production and scaling.

Existing Efforts

  • Available partner experience: A food-and-beverage developer explores uses for spent tea leaves and welcomes related collaborations. Assess its processing capabilities against the selected ingredient. A university bio-processed okara technology has an identified commercial route for soybean by-product ingredients and drinks.
  • First application: Choose one traceable input stream that meets agreed acceptance criteria. Establish volumes and handling, develop a limited product concept and assess taste, product stability, production yield, cost and customer interest.
  • Implementation priorities: Establish partner fit, ingredient specifications, capacity and commercial terms. Match tea- and okara-related capabilities to the specific input and product. Record planned fresh inputs separately from eligible surplus, and compare the complete economics with existing uses. Base waste-reduction and additional-profit claims on measured outcomes.

Potential Market Size

  • Global upcycled-food market: The global upcycled food products market was valued at US$41.71 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach US$79.51 billion by 2034, with 7.47% annual growth during 2026–2034. These figures provide broader market context; product development will assess customer demand for the selected ingredient and recipe.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
