Turn Maintenance Plans into Completed Work

Thematic Area: Smart Logistics and Asset Management

How might we turn maintenance procedures and logbook records into assigned, scheduled and verifiable work across ships and machinery?

Background

A shipping and logistics group wants stronger execution of asset-maintenance work. Maintenance logbooks already exist, but adherence and follow-through remain weak.

The objective is to connect asset records and standard procedures with task ownership, due dates, completion evidence and overdue follow-up. Relevant startup capabilities include asset-management systems, workflow software and mobile tools that help maintenance teams complete and document planned work.

Agree the specific assets, technical procedures and system connections with the operating team during project scoping.

Significance of Problem

  • Missed maintenance: Unclear ownership and escalation leave tasks incomplete.
  • Harder verification: Gaps in completion evidence prevent supervisors from checking that required work was completed correctly.
  • Greater disruption risk: Delayed attention to assets allows maintenance needs to affect operations.

Existing Efforts

  • Current company approach: Maintenance logbooks are already used. The identified gap is execution and adherence, so a solution must improve follow-through as well as record keeping.
  • Available capabilities: Configurable workflow software supports assignments, reminders, approvals and reporting. Road-fleet tools offer maintenance logs and servicing alerts for relevant vehicle classes.
  • Implementation priorities: Select an asset class, define responsibilities and test the workflow from scheduling through completion evidence and supervisor review. Assess maritime and machinery requirements explicitly, and select road-vehicle software for compatible asset classes and workflows.

Potential Market Size

  • The global enterprise asset management market is estimated at US$7.65 billion in 2024, reaching US$19.68 billion by 2030, with 17.2% annual growth during 2025–2030. Asia Pacific is forecast to grow at 18.5% annually over that period. This category covers asset management across industries.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
