Connect Solar Performance Data with Maintenance Action

Thematic Area: Climate and Clean Energy

How might we bring solar operating data and service records together so teams can identify issues and complete the right maintenance work?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business wants a solar-panel management and integration system to support its installed sites and future energy-service offering. It already offers operation and maintenance, and has explicitly requested improved monitoring and maintenance capabilities.

The objective is to connect usable generation data, electricity consumption, equipment records and maintenance actions in a workflow that technicians and service managers can use. Relevant startups offer energy monitoring, equipment integration, field-service tools or PV fault analysis.

Implementation priorities are to document the installed fleet, supported inverter brands, available measurements and existing systems, establish a service-performance baseline and select the workflow steps to improve.

Significance of Problem

  • Slower issue investigation: Separate operating and service records complicate investigation of abnormal performance.
  • Less consistent follow-through: Incomplete equipment and service histories make maintenance actions harder to assign and review.
  • Unclear customer value: Limited visibility of performance and completed work makes ongoing service benefits harder to explain.

Existing Efforts

  • Current company approach: Operation and maintenance is already offered. Use the existing service as the starting point to document the monitoring stack and establish a performance baseline.
  • Available partner capabilities: A solar monitoring provider combines generation, consumption, warranties and maintenance schedules, with supported building-system connections. A university research method combines device models and AI to investigate suspected PV faults.
  • Proposed collaboration: Connect one permitted site to a shared register, assign actions and review closure evidence. Test research fault indicators against known operating issues and maintenance outcomes. Establish equipment compatibility and Indonesian support, and apply technical assessment to monitoring alerts before maintenance decisions.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian rooftop installed base: Indonesia reported 916.99 MW of installed rooftop solar as of April 2026. This local asset base provides the sector context for monitoring and maintenance services.
  • Global installed base: Global PV capacity reached 2.96 TW at end-2025. This indicates the wider installed-asset base for ongoing solar services.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
