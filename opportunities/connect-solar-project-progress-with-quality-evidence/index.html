Connect Solar Project Progress with Quality Evidence

Thematic Area: Solar EPC Delivery & Quality Coordination

How might we give site and office teams a shared view of solar project progress, current documents and quality actions?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has named project management as a problem it wants to address. EPC represents over 90% of current revenue, making dependable execution central to the business.

The objective is to connect schedules, drawings, field reports and quality actions so site and office teams share a consistent view of delivery. Relevant startups offer construction-management software, mobile site reporting and document-control workflows.

The solution should make responsibilities, current document revisions, project progress and action closure easy to track across a selected execution stage.

Significance of Problem

Without an effective solution, the challenges include:

  • Harder project coordination: Disconnected site and office records make a consistent view of progress harder to maintain.
  • Slower action closure: Incomplete quality evidence and unclear responsibility delay the resolution of defects.
  • Document confusion: Teams spend additional time identifying which drawings, specifications and revisions apply to the work.

Existing Efforts

  • Business priority: Project management is one of the company’s identified needs.
  • Available partner capability: A construction-management provider combines schedules, budgets, site reports, quality evidence and project documents. Field teams record work and access revisions while managers review project information centrally.
  • Collaboration focus: Choose one project and execution stage. Assign responsibilities and introduce shared progress reporting, quality records and action tracking. Measure document retrieval and follow-up completion, including solar commissioning requirements.

Potential Market Size

  • Growing global installation activity: Approximately 690 GW of solar PV was installed globally in 2025, up 15% from 2024. This creates substantial project-delivery activity.
  • Indonesian project pipeline: Indonesia’s electricity plan announced on 26 May 2025 includes 17.1 GW of additional solar capacity during 2025–2034, supporting a significant planned solar-development pipeline.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
