Develop a Measurable Cooling as a Service Offering

Thematic Area: Climate and Clean Energy

How might we deliver reliable cooling through a service model with clear performance standards, ownership responsibilities and customer economics?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has explicitly identified Cooling as a Service as a technology and business-model interest. It wants to explore additional services that can strengthen its energy offering.

The objective is to assess a model in which customers pay for agreed cooling services while equipment ownership, maintenance and performance responsibilities are clearly assigned. Relevant partners provide efficient cooling equipment, controls, metering, asset financing or service-operation expertise.

Implementation priorities are to select the target customer and cooling application, document existing equipment, and define the relationship with the business’s solar offering. Set the funding structure and revenue model as part of pilot design.

Significance of Problem

  • Slower offering development: Unclear customer requirements and delivery responsibilities hold back a viable cooling-service proposition.
  • Difficult service pricing: A measured baseline and defined service output are essential for comparing costs and customer benefits.
  • Weak performance assurance: Incomplete maintenance and measurement arrangements make it difficult to demonstrate compliance with cooling commitments.

Existing Efforts

  • Company-stated need: Cooling as a Service is an explicit interest. Document existing cooling equipment, customer agreements and energy use to establish the pilot’s operating baseline.
  • Partner selection: Recruit a specialist cooling-service provider to supply cooling equipment and operating expertise, supported by solar monitoring, financing-preparation and workflow tools.
  • Proposed collaboration: Identify a suitable customer application and specialist partner. Establish cooling requirements, baseline energy use, metering, maintenance responsibilities and financing. Compare service payments with a conventional purchase using consistent assumptions. Measure cooling quality, availability, energy use and total cost in a controlled pilot before wider deployment.

Potential Market Size

  • Regional demand growth: Residential air-conditioner stocks in Southeast Asia are projected to triple by 2035 under today’s policy settings. This provides regional demand context for cooling services.
  • Broader service market: Global Energy as a Service revenue is forecast to reach US$222.7 billion by 2033. This includes several energy-service categories beyond cooling.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
