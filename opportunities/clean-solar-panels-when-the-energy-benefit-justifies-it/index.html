Clean Solar Panels When the Energy Benefit Justifies It

Thematic Area: Solar Cleaning Automation & Yield Protection

How might we automate solar-panel cleaning in a way that protects equipment and recovers enough energy to justify the operating cost?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has requested panel-cleaning technology to complement its installation and ongoing maintenance services.

The objective is to deploy cleaning where the energy recovered justifies the operating cost. Relevant startups offer cleaning robots, water-efficient systems, soiling measurement and scheduling tools.

The solution should fit roof layouts, panel specifications, site access and water availability while protecting equipment and supporting practical maintenance.

Significance of Problem

Without an effective solution, the challenges include:

  • Generation losses: Accumulated dirt reduces solar output and weakens the value delivered by affected installations.
  • Unnecessary service costs: Cleaning at unsuitable intervals consumes labour, water and equipment capacity without sufficient energy benefit.
  • Difficult operation: Cleaning equipment that does not fit the site or panel requirements is harder to operate and maintain.

Existing Efforts

  • Technology priority: Panel cleaning forms part of the business’s requested solar innovation capabilities.
  • Supporting capabilities: Use monitoring and inspection information to guide cleaning decisions. The collaboration combines these insights with specialist cleaning equipment and physical cleaning services.
  • Collaboration focus: Select a site with technical staff and run a controlled cleaning trial against a baseline, accounting for weather and operating conditions. Measure recovered generation, cleaning cost, resource use and equipment condition. Include manufacturer requirements and local maintenance support.

Potential Market Size

  • Economic case for cleaning: Soiling reduces global PV energy output by an estimated 4–7%, causing annual industry revenue losses of billions of euros.
  • Underlying asset base: Global PV capacity reached 2.96 TW at end-2025, providing a substantial installed base for cleaning services.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
