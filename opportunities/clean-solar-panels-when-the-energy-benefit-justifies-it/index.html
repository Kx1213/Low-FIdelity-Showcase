Clean Solar Panels When the Energy Benefit Justifies It

Thematic Area: Climate and Clean Energy

How might we automate solar-panel cleaning in a way that protects equipment and recovers enough energy to justify the operating cost?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has explicitly requested panel-cleaning technology as part of its solar innovation priorities. The aim is to develop a practical cleaning capability that complements installation and ongoing maintenance services.

The objective is to determine where automated cleaning is technically suitable and commercially worthwhile. Relevant startups offer cleaning robots, water-efficient systems, soiling measurement or tools that help schedule cleaning from observed site conditions.

Implementation priorities are to document the current cleaning process, measure site-specific soiling losses and select suitable equipment. Use roof layout, panel specifications, access, water availability and support arrangements as technology-selection criteria.

Significance of Problem

  • Avoidable generation losses: Accumulated dirt reduces energy output at sites affected by soiling.
  • Unnecessary service costs: Cleaning at unsuitable intervals consumes labour, water or equipment capacity without enough energy benefit.
  • Difficult deployment: Equipment that does not fit the site layout or panel requirements creates operating and maintenance difficulties.

Existing Efforts

  • Company-stated need: Panel-cleaning technology is requested. Establish a cleaning baseline and document existing equipment as the starting point for automation.
  • Partner selection: Recruit a specialist cleaning provider. Use monitoring and inspection tools to inform cleaning decisions alongside equipment that performs physical cleaning.
  • Proposed collaboration: Source a suitable cleaning partner and assess one site with technical staff. Compare a controlled cleaning trial with an appropriate baseline, allowing for weather and operating conditions. Evaluate recovered generation, cleaning cost, resource use and equipment condition. Confirm manufacturer requirements and maintenance support before scaling.

Potential Market Size

  • Economic case for cleaning: Soiling is estimated to reduce global PV energy output by 4–7%, causing annual industry revenue losses of billions of euros. This highlights the economic importance of managing solar-panel soiling.
  • Underlying asset base: Global PV capacity reached 2.96 TW at end-2025, providing a substantial installed base for site-specific cleaning assessment.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
