Match Fresh Stock to Demand Across Outlets and Channels

Thematic Area: Perishable Demand Planning & Inventory Allocation

How might we forecast demand and support reviewed replenishment and stock-allocation decisions before product quality deteriorates?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates multiple outlets and online channels. The opportunity is to connect demand planning with replenishment and stock allocation for perishable products.

The objective is to align purchasing, replenishment and transfers with demand, available stock and usable product life. Planning should include lead times, stock committed to orders and the cost of moving goods between locations.

Relevant startups offer demand prediction, inventory planning and configurable replenishment workflows. The collaboration combines historical sales and inventory records with practical planning rules and staff-led exception handling.

Significance of Problem

The key challenges are:

  • Unbalanced availability: Poorly aligned demand and allocation create missed sales at one outlet and excess stock at another.
  • Higher inventory costs: Early replenishment and unnecessary transfers tie up cash and add handling costs.
  • Short selling windows: Perishable-stock decisions must account for product dates, condition and remaining selling time.

Existing Efforts

  • Available workflow capability: Configurable inventory software supports shared stock records, replenishment proposals, reservations and transfers using agreed rules and staff approvals.
  • Forecasting integration: Connect a suitable demand-forecasting capability with inventory workflows. Compare model outputs with a simple baseline using actual records, including unavailable-stock periods and promotions.
  • Collaboration focus: Select one category across participating outlets and compare recommendations with current planning decisions. Measure forecast error, availability, waste, transfer cost and working capital. Assign responsibility for exceptions, quality decisions and stock movements.

Potential Market Size

  • Global inventory-software market: The global inventory management software market was valued at US$3.7 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach US$7.1 billion by 2033, with 8.9% annual growth during 2026–2033. The market covers inventory-management applications across industries.

  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
