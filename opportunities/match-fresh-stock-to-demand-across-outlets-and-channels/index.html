Match Fresh Stock to Demand Across Outlets and Channels

Thematic Area: Food Innovation and Sustainability

How might we forecast demand and support reviewed replenishment and stock-allocation decisions before product quality deteriorates?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates multiple outlets and online channels. This project focuses on demand forecasting and stock allocation, using forecast accuracy, stockouts and excess stock by location to set improvement priorities.

The objective is to align purchasing, replenishment and transfers with demand, available stock and usable product life. Planning must account for lead times, stock already committed to orders and the cost of moving goods between locations.

Relevant startup capabilities include demand prediction, inventory planning and configurable replenishment workflows. Document existing decision rules, historical data and operating constraints, then select a workflow with a measurable improvement target.

Significance of Problem

  • Unbalanced availability: Poor alignment between demand and allocation creates missed sales in one outlet and excess stock in another.
  • Higher inventory costs: Early replenishment and unnecessary transfers tie up cash and add handling costs without improving availability.
  • Weaker decisions for perishables: Plans that ignore product dates, condition and remaining selling time produce impractical recommendations.

Existing Efforts

  • Available workflow capability: Configurable inventory software supports shared stock records, replenishment proposals, reservations and transfers using agreed rules and staff approvals. Use these capabilities as the operating foundation and evaluate forecasting performance separately for perishable foods.
  • Forecasting integration: Select or integrate a forecasting capability, test it on the retailer’s actual records and compare it with a simple baseline. Include unavailable-stock periods and promotions where supported by the data.
  • First application: Choose one category across selected outlets, agree planning rules and compare recommendations with current decisions before live use. Measure forecast error alongside availability, waste, transfer cost and working capital. Assign staff clear responsibility for exceptions, quality decisions and the release of stock movements.

Potential Market Size

  • Global inventory-software market: The global inventory management software market was valued at US$3.7 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach US$7.1 billion by 2033, with 8.9% annual growth during 2026–2033. These figures span several industries and provide broader context for perishable-food demand forecasting.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
