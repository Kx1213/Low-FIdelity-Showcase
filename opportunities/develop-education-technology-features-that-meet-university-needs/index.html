Develop Education Technology Features That Meet University Needs

Thematic Area: Education and Learning Innovation

How might we integrate additional learning or administrative features that create measurable value for university customers?

Background

An education technology business wants to expand its product offering. It needs to determine which customer problems are most valuable to solve and which partner technologies fit its existing platform.

The objective is to prioritise a feature, establish customer demand and test its value before wider development. Target areas include learning support, language practice, programming assistance, learning analytics and education administration.

Document the current product architecture, preferred customer segment, Indonesian-language requirements and integration needs during project scoping.

Significance of Problem

  • Unmet customer needs: Gaps in the product leave important teaching, learning and administrative activities underserved.
  • Lower commercial value: Limited product coverage weakens institutions’ reasons to adopt, renew and expand use.
  • Unproductive development spending: Building features without sufficient demand and a workable integration approach reduces the return on development investment.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: Education platforms offer AI-supported learning, lecture tools, learning activities, analytics, administration and single sign-on. Other tools support language practice and AI-assisted writing and speaking assessment. A university programming-tutor prototype compares student programs with instructor references and test cases.
  • Implementation priorities: Select one institutional need and test the relevant capability through a limited integration pilot. Include course-specific testing and educator review for research technologies. Evaluate usefulness, correctness, adoption, integration effort and willingness to pay.

Potential Market Size

  • The global learning management system market is estimated at US$34.1 billion in 2026, reaching US$123.8 billion by 2033, with 20.2% annual growth during 2026–2033. The category covers academic and corporate applications. Size individual feature opportunities using the selected customer segment and pricing.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
