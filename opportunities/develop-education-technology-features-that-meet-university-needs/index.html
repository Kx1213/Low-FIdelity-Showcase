Develop Education Technology Features That Meet University Needs

Thematic Area: Education

How might we integrate additional learning or administrative features that create measurable value for university customers?

Background

Expanding an education technology offering requires identifying the customer problems most valuable to solve and assessing partner technologies that fit an existing platform.

The objective is to prioritise a feature, establish customer demand and test its value before wider development. Target areas include learning support, language practice, programming assistance, learning analytics and education administration.

Document the current product architecture, preferred customer segment, Indonesian-language requirements and integration needs during project scoping.

Significance of the Problem

  • Unmet customer needs: Gaps in the product leave important teaching, learning and administrative activities underserved.
  • Lower commercial value: Limited product coverage weakens institutions’ reasons to adopt, renew and expand use.
  • Unproductive development spending: Building features without sufficient demand and a workable integration approach reduces the return on development investment.

Potential Market Size

  • Global education-technology market: Grand View Research estimates the global market at US$187.0 billion in 2025, reaching US$437.5 billion by 2033. This covers schools, universities and other learning markets.
  • Learning-management systems: Grand View Research estimates the global LMS market at US$34.1 billion in 2026, reaching US$123.8 billion by 2033. This includes academic and corporate platforms and provides market context for integrated learning features.
  • Indonesian higher-education users: Indonesia's 2025 statistics recorded 10.75 million enrolled students and 328,241 lecturers, providing a large user base for university learning support, teaching tools and administrative features.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.

Market Validation

Develop Education Technology Features That Meet University Needs

  • Universities use platforms for several functions: Atma Jaya Catholic University of Indonesia uses Coursera for credit-bearing student learning, supplementary teaching materials and staff development. This shows that a university customer can adopt a platform across several departments when its features address distinct learning and operational needs. (Source: Coursera (opens in a new tab))
  • Integrated support reaches teaching staff at scale: VU Amsterdam used in-app guidance inside Canvas to reach nearly 2,200 teachers and improve the structure of hundreds of courses. The deployment demonstrates practical value from adding guidance and support features to a university's existing learning platform. (Source: Instructure (opens in a new tab))
  • Specialist learning features attract substantial use: Instructure's 2026 US higher-education analysis recorded more than 9 million Zoom users, 8.59 million Kaltura users and 8.33 million Panopto users through Canvas learning-tool integrations. These overlapping audiences demonstrate established use of connected collaboration and video features alongside the core learning platform. (Source: Instructure (opens in a new tab))
