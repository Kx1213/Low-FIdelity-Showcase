Find Profitable Opportunities to Grow Delivery Revenue

Thematic Area: Market Access and Business Growth

How might we identify underserved delivery needs and turn them into profitable growth in customers, orders and revenue?

Background

A delivery business wants to increase revenue alongside improvements in operating efficiency. It needs to determine which customer groups, service areas and delivery needs offer the strongest growth opportunities.

The objective is to identify a specific commercial opportunity and test its profitability. Approaches include analysing demand patterns, comparing service coverage with customer locations and assessing additional services.

Define the priority customer segment, sales process, available data and target outcomes during project scoping.

Significance of Problem

  • Missed revenue: Unidentified customers and delivery needs limit sales opportunities.
  • Inefficient expansion: Investment in locations with insufficient demand and unattractive delivery economics reduces returns.
  • Weaker commercial decisions: Limited evidence makes it harder to prioritise services, customer groups and sales activity.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: A geospatial platform provides location data, heatmaps and spatial analysis for assessing service coverage and prioritising areas for demand analysis.
  • Implementation priorities: Select a demand-growth solution that combines geographic analysis with customer, order and commercial information. Test a focused growth hypothesis using customer conversion, incremental revenue and delivery profitability as success measures.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesia’s e-commerce market was projected to reach US$71 billion in gross merchandise value in 2025, growing by more than 14% from 2024. This provides context for demand for goods movement and related delivery services. E-commerce GMV measures the value of goods sold; delivery-market sizing must use service demand and pricing within the selected customer segment.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
