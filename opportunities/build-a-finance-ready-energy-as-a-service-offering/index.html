Build a Finance-Ready Energy as a Service Offering

Thematic Area: Climate and Clean Energy

How might we establish a viable ownership, financing and service model that turns solar installations into reliable recurring energy services?

Background

An Indonesian solar engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) business wants to expand into Energy as a Service. EPC currently accounts for over 90% of its revenue, and the company has ranked Energy as a Service as its highest strategic priority.

The objective is to define who owns the solar assets, how investment is funded and how customers pay for ongoing services. An asset-owner model requires a credible financial case, customer contracts and reliable installation and maintenance arrangements.

Relevant partners include providers of solar project finance, asset-development partnerships, financial modelling and due-diligence workflows. Implementation priorities are to select the ownership structure, size the investment requirement and define the immediate financing need.

Significance of Problem

  • Slower business-model development: Unclear ownership and funding responsibilities hold back suitable service opportunities.
  • Difficult investment assessment: Incomplete project information makes it harder for prospective funders to assess costs, risks and expected returns.
  • Inconsistent service pricing: An agreed asset and service model is essential for pricing contracts and operating costs consistently.

Existing Efforts

  • Current company direction: The company has identified financing and joint-venture exploration as future directions. Build on this direction by documenting existing asset-financing arrangements and selecting the Energy as a Service structure.
  • Available partner capability: A secure data-room provider can organise approved designs, contracts and financial documents for prospective lenders or investors. It supports due diligence and does not provide capital.
  • Proposed collaboration: Select one ownership model and candidate project, prepare its investment case and identify suitable capital partners. Confirm local contracting and financing requirements before testing a proposal. Distinguish investor funding of assets from customer payments generating recurring revenue.

Potential Market Size

  • Global service opportunity: The global Energy as a Service market is forecast to reach US$222.7 billion by 2033, growing 13.9% annually during 2026–2033. This includes energy supply, maintenance and efficiency services beyond solar.
  • Indonesian development pipeline: Indonesia’s 2025–2034 electricity plan includes 17.1 GW of additional solar capacity. This planned power-sector pipeline provides sector context. Select projects that match the business’s capabilities and commercial requirements.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
