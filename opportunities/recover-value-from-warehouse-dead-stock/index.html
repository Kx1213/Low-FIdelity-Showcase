Recover Value from Warehouse Dead Stock

Thematic Area: Logistic & Shipping / Property

Problem Statement: How might we connect suitable warehouse dead stock with relevant buyers to recover value and release storage capacity?

Background

Recovering value from warehouse dead stock requires identifying goods eligible for sale, choosing suitable sales channels and coordinating the clearance process.

The objective is to turn authorised inventory into recoverable value while considering condition, pricing and fulfilment costs. Relevant partner capabilities include liquidation channels, wholesale marketplaces, multichannel commerce software and business-to-business sales tools.

Significance of the Problem

  • Continued storage pressure: Unsold stock occupies space needed for other warehouse activities.
  • Lower value recovery: Deteriorating condition and changing commercial relevance reduce the value of ageing goods.
  • Slower sales conversion: Fragmented listings and buyer follow-up leave suitable stock without a clear route to sale.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian warehouse-service context: Mordor Intelligence estimates Indonesia’s third-party warehousing market at US$3.57 billion in 2026, reaching US$5.51 billion by 2031. This identifies the surrounding customer industry rather than the value of warehouse dead stock or potential resale revenue.
  • Concentrated warehouse base: Greater Jakarta had approximately 3.5 million square metres of modern warehouse space, with 97.4% occupancy in Q2 2026. This provides an operating base for clearance services. A defensible dead-stock sales estimate still requires inventory-level evidence on ownership, condition, recoverable prices and sell-through.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.

Market Validation

Recover Value from Warehouse Dead Stock

  • An Indonesian clearance marketplace already operates: In July 2025, Surplus Indonesia reported more than 400,000 products recovered, over 1 million users and more than US$1 million in potential losses avoided. Its categories include food, clothing and household goods. These are company-reported cumulative figures rather than annual warehouse dead-stock sales. (Source: Surplus Indonesia (opens in a new tab))
  • Retailers use additional clearance channels: Atria’s testimonial in the same announcement describes Surplus as a new distribution channel for clearance products and a way to reach additional customers. This supports the buyer-access proposition without quantifying recovery rates or sales uplift. (Source: Surplus Indonesia (opens in a new tab))
