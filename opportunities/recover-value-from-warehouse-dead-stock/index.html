Recover Value from Warehouse Dead Stock

Thematic Area: Logistic & Shipping / Property

How might we connect suitable warehouse dead stock with relevant buyers to recover value and release storage capacity?

Background

Recovering value from warehouse dead stock requires identifying goods eligible for sale, choosing suitable sales channels and coordinating the clearance process.

The objective is to turn authorised inventory into recoverable value while considering condition, pricing and fulfilment costs. Relevant partner capabilities include liquidation channels, wholesale marketplaces, multichannel commerce software and business-to-business sales tools.

Significance of the Problem

  • Continued storage pressure: Unsold stock occupies space needed for other warehouse activities.
  • Lower value recovery: Deteriorating condition and changing commercial relevance reduce the value of ageing goods.
  • Slower sales conversion: Fragmented listings and buyer follow-up leave suitable stock without a clear route to sale.

Potential Market Size

  • Global reverse-logistics market: Grand View Research estimates this broader market at US$954.9 billion in 2026, reaching US$3.18 trillion by 2033. It covers services for returned goods and other reverse flows, including transport and warehousing.
  • Global overstock problem: IHL Group estimated the annual cost of retail overstocks at US$572 billion in 2025, highlighting the value at stake in clearing excess inventory. This measures overstock losses, rather than resale revenue or the value of all dead stock.
  • Established surplus sales: Liquidity Services processed US$1.57 billion in surplus-marketplace transactions in FY2025, up 15%. This is one operator’s gross merchandise value across multiple asset categories.

Market evidence

Market Validation

Recover Value from Warehouse Dead Stock

  • A substantial buyer base already exists: Liquidity Services reported 6.4 million cumulative registered buyers as of 30 June 2026 and approximately 334,000 completed auction transactions during the quarter, with transactions up 17% year on year. Its retail, industrial and public-sector marketplaces demonstrate established demand for surplus goods and assets. (Source: Liquidity Services (opens in a new tab))
  • Better buyer access improves recovery: Liquidity Services reports that Ferguson doubled its recovery rate compared with previous liquidation channels and completed more than 1,800 auctions from over 250 locations. Selling directly from branches reduced extra handling and connected stock with a wider pool of buyers. (Source: Liquidity Services — Ferguson (opens in a new tab))
  • Multiple sales channels move more stock: In its 2022 Gildan case study, Liquidity Services reported four times the recovery and ten times the average sales within six months. Combining online auctions with direct truckload sales helped the apparel manufacturer clear excess inventory while protecting recovery value as volumes increased. (Source: Liquidity Services — Gildan (opens in a new tab))
