Develop Suitable and Appealing Prepared-Food Packaging

Thematic Area: Food Innovation and Sustainability

How might we improve prepared-food packaging presentation and select alternative materials that fit the product, handling and preparation conditions?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates ready-to-eat, ready-to-heat and ready-to-cook lines. This project addresses attractive packaging through improvements to materials, format and presentation. The company will define detailed product and packaging requirements during pilot planning.

The objective is to develop a pack that presents the food clearly, suits customer use and performs through the intended storage and distribution conditions. Environmental claims and material-performance claims must be supported by relevant evidence.

Relevant startup capabilities include packaging materials, structural design, printing, sealing and manufacturing services. Select a representative product and document the current pack, filling method and intended preparation conditions before choosing a material.

Significance of Problem

  • Weak product presentation: Unclear packaging information makes purchasing and preparation more difficult for customers.
  • Packaging incompatibility: An unsuitable material or seal fails during filling, handling, storage or intended preparation.
  • Poor investment returns: Material changes without evidence on cost, machinability and performance increase expense without delivering the intended benefit.

Existing Efforts

  • Available material capability: A packaging-material developer produces biodegradable films using agricultural residues and publishes material specifications. These specifications provide the starting point for selecting samples and testing food-contact suitability and performance for the retailer’s products.
  • First application: Select one product and create samples in an appropriate format. Compare appearance, information clarity, handling, seal quality and unit cost against the existing pack, including checks under intended storage and use conditions.
  • Implementation priorities: Establish sample supply, food-contact documentation, barrier properties, sealing compatibility and any heating or freezing requirements. Conduct product-specific shelf-life testing as a separate workstream. Assign packaging design, conversion and testing responsibilities across the partners needed to deliver the complete pack.

Potential Market Size

  • Global food-packaging market: The global food packaging market was valued at US$421.6 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach US$599.0 billion by 2033, with 4.3% annual growth during 2026–2033. Asia Pacific represented 40.0% of 2025 revenue. These figures cover all food-packaging materials and formats.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
