Strengthen Carbon Measurement, Reporting and Verification

Thematic Area: Energy & Renewable Energy / Climate & Sustainability

How might we improve measurement, reporting and verification across carbon projects to produce consistent, traceable evidence of emissions reductions and removals while reducing monitoring costs and reporting delays?

Background

Reliable evidence of carbon-project performance depends on consistent data collection, analysis and documentation to support independent verification.

The objective is to establish a repeatable MRV process covering baseline information, ongoing monitoring, emissions calculations and supporting records across existing and future projects. Solutions should help project teams maintain consistent data and give independent validators and verifiers access to the evidence they need.

Relevant startup capabilities include environmental monitoring, remote sensing, geospatial analysis, carbon accounting and reporting automation. Nature-based projects are the main focus; other project types are welcome, with blue carbon excluded.

Significance of the Problem

  • Verification delays: Incomplete baseline data, monitoring records and supporting evidence slow assessment and verification.
  • Higher monitoring costs: Repeated data collection and separate reporting processes increase the cost of managing small projects.
  • Weaker credibility: Inconsistent methods and limited data traceability make reported outcomes harder to substantiate.
  • Slower portfolio growth: MRV processes that require substantial manual effort for each additional project constrain expansion.

Potential Market Size

  • Global carbon-credit market: MSCI estimates the primary global carbon-credit market at US$1.4 billion in 2025 and projects it to reach US$5–20 billion by 2030.
  • Digital MRV market: Meticulous Research estimates the carbon-credit digital MRV market at US$300–500 million in 2026, covering software, satellite and sensor data, and digital verification services.
  • Verification and certification services: MarketsandMarkets estimates this segment at US$160 million in 2025, growing to US$450 million by 2030. These services check project results and support credit certification.

The digital MRV and verification estimates overlap and should not be added together.

Market Validation

Strengthen Carbon Measurement, Reporting and Verification

  • Large existing project base: More than 10,200 carbon projects were registered across 18 major registries by the end of 2025, issuing 294 million credits during the year. These projects need reliable measurement, reporting and verification. (Source: MSCI (opens in a new tab))
  • Digital MRV is already delivering credits: In February 2026, Verra approved its first credits under a digital MRV pilot using fully digital verification of solar-project monitoring data. The pilot supports monthly or bimonthly credit issuance, demonstrating practical adoption by a major carbon standard. (Source: Verra (opens in a new tab))
  • A clear benefit for developers: In March 2026, Gold Standard announced its first fully digital cookstove-credit issuance with ATEC Global. Connected devices track stove use, independent auditors check the data, and developers can request credit issuance every 90 days through the programme’s interim track. (Source: Gold Standard (opens in a new tab))
