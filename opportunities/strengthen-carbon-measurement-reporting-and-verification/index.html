Strengthen Carbon Measurement, Reporting and Verification

Thematic Area: Carbon Data and Assurance

How might we improve measurement, reporting and verification across carbon projects to produce consistent, traceable evidence of emissions reductions and removals while reducing monitoring costs and reporting delays?

Background

Reliable evidence of carbon-project performance depends on consistent data collection, analysis and documentation to support independent verification.

The objective is to establish a repeatable MRV process covering baseline information, ongoing monitoring, emissions calculations and supporting records across existing and future projects. Solutions should help project teams maintain consistent data and give independent validators and verifiers access to the evidence they need.

Relevant startup capabilities include environmental monitoring, remote sensing, geospatial analysis, carbon accounting and reporting automation. Nature-based projects are the main focus; other project types are welcome, with blue carbon excluded.

Significance of the Problem

  • Verification delays: Incomplete baseline data, monitoring records and supporting evidence slow assessment and verification.
  • Higher monitoring costs: Repeated data collection and separate reporting processes increase the cost of managing small projects.
  • Weaker credibility: Inconsistent methods and limited data traceability make reported outcomes harder to substantiate.
  • Slower portfolio growth: MRV processes that require substantial manual effort for each additional project constrain expansion.

Potential Market Size

  • Growing assurance-services market: Grand View Research’s September 2026 report estimates the global carbon-credit validation, verification and certification market at US$366.6 million in 2025, reaching US$2.07 billion by 2033, with 24.2% annual growth during 2026–2033. This broader services market provides context for MRV demand; it includes activities beyond digital MRV software.
  • Regulatory support in Indonesia: Forestry Ministerial Regulation No. 6 of 2026, explained by the Ministry on 29 April 2026, establishes a process covering measurement, carbon-unit calculation, validation, verification and national registration for forestry carbon trading. These requirements support demand for reliable project data and reporting tools that assist independent assessment.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.
