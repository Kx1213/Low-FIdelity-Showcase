Connect Order Intake with Store Fulfilment

Thematic Area: Retail Workflow Automation & Order Coordination

How might we connect order capture, stock checks, picking and packing to reduce avoidable administrative work in a selected store workflow?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer serves customers through physical stores and online channels. The opportunity is to connect order intake with store fulfilment through a shared workflow.

The objective is to link order capture, stock checks, picking, packing and handover with clear task ownership. A shared order record gives staff a practical view of progress and outstanding actions.

Relevant startups offer workflow automation, information capture and system integration. The collaboration focuses on a selected store process, its order sources, staff responsibilities and exception handling.

Significance of Problem

The key challenges are:

  • Repeated administrative work: Repeated entry across systems consumes staff time and creates additional record-checking work.
  • Unclear order status: Disconnected handovers make it harder to see which orders have been checked, picked, packed or released.
  • More difficult scaling: Unclear ownership and fragmented exception handling add supervision demands as order volumes grow.

Existing Efforts

  • Available service capability: A tailored software and workflow-automation provider supports order capture, information validation and the movement of records between business processes.
  • First application: Map one store workflow using representative orders. Connect order capture, stock checks and fulfilment tasks, with staff review for exceptions and a clear record of each handover.
  • Collaboration focus: Connect relevant systems, product identifiers, stock updates and staff permissions. Compare processing time, corrections and completed orders per staff hour across the workflow, including picking and packing where these steps are integrated.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian retail operating context: Indonesia recorded approximately US$106.2 billion in grocery retail sales in 2025. This provides a substantial customer-industry base for retail workflow and order-coordination tools.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
