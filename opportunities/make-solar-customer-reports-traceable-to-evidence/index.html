Make Solar Customer Reports Traceable to Evidence

Thematic Area: Digital Operations and Automation

How might we prepare clear solar customer reports whose performance figures and claims can be traced to approved supporting records?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business publishes project information and offers maintenance reporting. This project focuses on strengthening the process for preparing and reviewing customer performance evidence.

The objective is to map report preparation and evidence review, select the workflow to improve and test a defined customer-report format. Relevant startups offer document review, evidence tracing or numerical consistency checks.

Report content can include generation, maintenance actions and clearly stated savings assumptions. Define the audience, select supporting records and obtain customer permission. Use reporting tools to support review, with approved methods and technical sign-off for performance and impact claims.

Significance of Problem

  • Harder claim assessment: Unclear underlying evidence and assumptions make performance statements difficult for customers to assess.
  • More review effort: Inconsistent figures across drafts and supporting records require additional checking.
  • Limited reuse of results: Incomplete approvals or evidence make reports harder to use in renewals, references or customer communications.

Existing Efforts

  • Implementation priorities: Agree the report format and commercial use, map the supporting evidence and select the reporting workflow before choosing technology.
  • Available partner capabilities: An AI document-review provider traces review findings to source passages and supports reviewer comments and sign-off. A university numerical-document research method provides a development route for comparing reported quantities with supporting records. Use technical measurement and approved calculation methods to verify energy generation, savings and emissions alongside document checks.
  • Proposed collaboration: Select one permitted report, identify supporting records and test reviewers’ ability to trace and resolve its claims. Obtain technical approval for calculations, periods and assumptions, and customer approval for the intended external use. Complete development and testing before incorporating research-based numerical checking into operational review.

Potential Market Size

  • Expanding solar reporting base: Global installed PV capacity reached 2.96 TW at end-2025. This provides a growing asset base for customer performance reporting.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
