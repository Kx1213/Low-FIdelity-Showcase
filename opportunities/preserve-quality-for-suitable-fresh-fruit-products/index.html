Preserve Quality for Suitable Fresh-Fruit Products

Thematic Area: Food Innovation and Sustainability

How might we preserve the quality of compatible fresh fruit for longer under the retailer’s actual storage and handling conditions?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer handles fresh produce alongside other grocery and prepared-food categories. This project focuses on fresh-fruit preservation, using fruit-specific loss and quality measurements to select a practical intervention.

The objective is to improve saleable quality for a selected fruit at an acceptable additional cost. The trial must reflect the actual maturity, supply route, storage conditions and handling practices involved.

Relevant partner capabilities include edible coatings, post-harvest treatment, application equipment and produce-quality assessment. Establish compatibility for each fruit individually and keep performance claims specific to the tested fruit and conditions.

Significance of Problem

  • Reduced saleable quality: Deterioration leaves fruit in an unacceptable condition before its intended sale.
  • Lower value recovery: A short effective selling period creates markdown pressure or unsold stock when demand and timing do not align.
  • Unproductive treatment costs: A coating unsuited to the fruit or storage conditions adds handling and material costs without a commercial benefit.

Existing Efforts

  • Available research capability: A university plant-derived edible-coating technology regulates gas exchange and provides antibacterial and antifungal protection for compatible fruits, including mangoes, avocados and papayas. The technology is reported at TRL6 with laboratory and field testing. Use fruit-specific trials to establish its performance within the retailer’s supply chain.
  • First application: Choose a compatible fruit and compare treated and untreated batches with similar maturity and handling. Review quality over time, acceptable selling days, customer-relevant appearance and taste, application effort and net value recovered.
  • Implementation priorities: Establish a fruit-specific formulation, application arrangements, supply capacity and relevant food-use documentation. Agree acceptance criteria with the quality team and maintain the intended storage controls. Limit commercial shelf-life claims to tested fruits and conditions. Treat vegetables, meat, seafood and prepared meals as separate technology-selection projects.

Potential Market Size

  • Global produce-loss context: Fruits and vegetables experienced an estimated 25.4% loss worldwide in 2023 across stages after harvest and before retail. This figure describes the broader preservation challenge, while the pilot establishes the company’s fruit-specific retail baseline and commercial value.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
