Create a Suitable Financing Route for Solar Customers

Thematic Area: Market Access and Business Growth

How might we help suitable customers finance a solar-system purchase through a locally available and commercially viable financing arrangement?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has expressed interest in financing and joint-venture exploration. Customer purchase financing is a distinct route to develop alongside financing for company-owned energy-service assets.

The objective is to identify customer projects facing upfront purchase-cost barriers and select a financing partner for that segment. Relevant partners offer solar loans, instalment arrangements, credit assessment or EPC-linked finance workflows.

Implementation priorities are to distinguish customer financing from the business’s corporate and project-finance needs, define customer type and system size, and establish ownership, local availability and indicative terms before presenting a financing offer.

Significance of Problem

  • Unconverted customer demand: Upfront purchase-cost barriers prevent suitable projects from progressing without an appropriate payment route.
  • Unclear customer decisions: Inconsistent financing assumptions make total purchase costs and repayment obligations difficult to compare.
  • Difficult partner selection: Financing products must match Indonesian customers’ market conditions, system sizes and eligibility requirements.

Existing Efforts

  • Company-stated direction: Financing is a named interest. Define the borrower, financing mechanism and purchase barrier as the starting point for partner selection.
  • Available partner capability: A Vietnam-focused clean-energy finance provider connects financed purchases with EPC partners and supports installation safeguards and repayment administration. Partner selection must establish an Indonesian delivery route and terms suited to the target commercial project sizes.
  • Proposed collaboration: Choose one customer segment facing a purchase-cost barrier and design the financing route around its requirements. Select a locally available lending or financing route and document eligibility, ownership, installation acceptance and total payment terms. Structure the EPC business’s revenue around agreed system, installation and service fees. Allocate repayments and interest according to the financing contract and record them under the correct recipient.

Potential Market Size

  • Local solar customer context: Indonesia reported 916.99 MW of installed rooftop solar in April 2026. This indicates an established local sector; financing demand depends on the selected customers’ purchase needs, eligibility and ability to repay.
  • Capital-intensive market: Global solar-project investment is estimated at US$365 billion in 2026. This provides broader capital-investment context for solar financing.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
