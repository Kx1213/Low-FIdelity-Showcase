Test Flexible Robotics for Repetitive Food Handling

Thematic Area: Digital Operations and Automation

How might we automate a suitable repetitive food-handling task while maintaining product integrity, hygiene and a practical production cycle time?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates prepared-food lines using ingredients also sold through its retail business. This project focuses on improving production efficiency through a repetitive food-handling task selected with the production team.

The objective is to identify one handling, positioning or assembly task where automation delivers measurable value. A workable solution must accommodate representative variations in food shape, texture, moisture and fragility under realistic conditions.

Relevant partner capabilities include robotic grippers, machine vision, robot integration and food-production engineering. Pilot planning will document task volumes, manual effort, cleaning procedures and acceptable product-damage rates.

Significance of Problem

  • Poor automation decisions: Equipment spending without a task-specific feasibility test leads to limited operating value or substantial redesign.
  • Capacity constraints: Repetitive manual bottlenecks limit the output achievable from the wider production process.
  • Inconsistent handling: An unsuitable automated approach damages products or adds interruptions, reducing the productivity benefit.

Existing Efforts

  • Available research: An adjustable robotic gripper research prototype uses configurable fingers, adjustable stiffness and interchangeable gripping mechanisms for foods with different characteristics. At TRL3, it provides an early proof of concept for integration into a production-cell trial.
  • First application: Choose one product and one task. Compare grasp reliability, placement accuracy, cycle time, product damage and cleaning effort against the current method using representative samples.
  • Implementation priorities: Integrate the prototype with a robot and controls, assess food-contact materials, incorporate hygienic design and validate cleaning procedures. A specialist integrator and the company’s quality team must establish a suitable test setup before production use. Qualify additional products and tasks through separate trials before broadening the application.

Potential Market Size

  • Global food-robotics market: The global food robotics market was valued at US$3.07 billion in 2025 and is forecast to reach US$13.16 billion by 2035, with 15.7% annual growth during 2026–2035. These figures cover several robot types and food applications, providing broader context for flexible grippers.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
