Test Flexible Robotics for Repetitive Food Handling

Thematic Area: Food Robotics & Handling Automation

How might we automate a suitable repetitive food-handling task while maintaining product integrity, hygiene and a practical production cycle time?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates prepared-food lines using ingredients also sold through its retail business. The opportunity is to automate a suitable repetitive handling task within food production.

The objective is to select a handling, positioning or assembly task and evaluate automation against product integrity, hygiene and production cycle time. The solution should accommodate the shape, texture, moisture and fragility of representative products.

Relevant partners offer robotic grippers, machine vision, robot integration and food-production engineering. The collaboration combines practical task design with the operating and cleaning requirements of the production line.

Significance of Problem

The key challenges are:

  • Equipment selection: A clear task specification helps avoid unsuitable equipment choices and costly production-cell redesign.
  • Capacity constraints: Repetitive handling tasks can restrict production throughput and absorb staff time.
  • Product damage and interruptions: Gripping and placement errors damage products, interrupt production and reduce the value of automation.

Existing Efforts

  • Available research: An adjustable robotic gripper research prototype uses configurable fingers, adjustable stiffness and interchangeable gripping mechanisms for foods with different characteristics. The technology is at TRL3, an early proof of concept.
  • First application: Choose one product and one task. Compare grasp reliability, placement accuracy, cycle time, product damage and cleaning effort against the current method using representative samples.
  • Collaboration focus: Integrate the prototype with a robot and controls. Work with a specialist integrator and the quality team on food-contact materials, hygienic design, cleaning procedures and a task-specific production setup.

Potential Market Size

  • Global food-robotics market: The global food robotics market was valued at US$3.07 billion in 2025 and is forecast to reach US$13.16 billion by 2035, with 15.7% annual growth during 2026–2035. The market includes several robot types and food applications.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
