Fraud & Theft Mitigation: Precision Asset Tracking, AI Site Security, and Inline Quality Verification

Thematic Area: Site Security

How might we prevent crude oil theft and product adulteration through integrated asset tracking, site monitoring, and continuous quality verification solutions to eliminate undetected inventory losses and secure supply chain integrity across remote extraction and transfer points?

Background

Improvement is needed to address the difficulty of identifying and quantifying physical losses of crude oil, unauthorized site access, equipment tampering, and product quality changes or adulteration during custody transfer operations.

The goal is to deploy an integrated monitoring system that simultaneously tracks physical asset movements, detects unauthorized human/vehicle activity, and verifies fuel quality during transfer. The objective is to establish immediate visibility over volume discrepancies, secure remote extraction/storage sites, and reconcile both quantity and quality changes at critical transfer points to eliminate undetected inventory losses and secure supply chain integrity.

The work involves site operations and compliance leads, security personnel, transport managers and custody transfer auditors across remote extraction sites, storage facilities, equipment yards and transfer points. Monitoring covers daily operations, equipment movements, vehicle dispatches and batch transfers.

The approach equips remote sites and transfer points with precision geofenced asset tracking, AI-powered computer vision safety/security analytics, and inline continuous fuel quality sensors working in parallel to secure supply chain integrity. This combines commercial upstream operational and compliance requirements with ecosystem startups specializing in industrial asset tracking, computer vision security, and inline fluid quality analytics.

Significance of the Problem

  • Inventory Shrinkage & Financial Loss: Severe, unquantified financial losses from stolen crude oil stock, missing equipment, and tampered fuel batches during transport and storage.
  • Site Security & Worker Hazards: Unauthorized site access, intruder incursions, and equipment tampering compromising worker safety, facility operational continuity, and physical infrastructure at remote sites.
  • Supply Chain Degradation: Contractual, legal, and reputational damage when adulterated or off-spec crude oil reaches trading partners due to undetected quality changes during custody transfer.
  • Remote Connectivity Constraints: Deploying continuous site monitoring and continuous quality verification systems in remote extraction areas with low or zero internet connectivity presents technical transmission challenges.
  • Dual-Vector Blindspots: Quality monitoring alone cannot verify physical volume theft unless paired directly with asset tracking and volume reconciliation, while physical site monitoring alone misses deliberate chemical adulteration during active product transfer.

Potential Market Size

  • Market size and growth: The global energy supply chain loses over USD 133 Billion annually to illicit fuel trade, theft, and inventory shrinkage, while the global asset tracking market is valued between USD 21.3 Billion and USD 28.9 Billion. This drives high demand for real-time asset tracking, computer vision monitoring, and continuous quality verification technologies across remote extraction and logistics points.
  • Supporting factors: Mandatory regulatory sign-offs and compliance reviews (e.g., KSO/K3S compliance mandates in Indonesia), strict corporate audit requirements for custody transfer integrity, zero-tolerance loss prevention policies across upstream assets.

Qualification & Fitness

  • Technology Readiness: Ready commercial technologies and pilot-proven products exist today for deployment, requiring field testing to confirm performance under remote, low/zero internet conditions.
  • NUS Linkage: Built by research teams at the National University of Singapore (NUS) or supported through the BLOCK71 network, Technology Innovation Programme (TIG), or National GRIP Programme. Open to NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs), professors, as well as local and international technology providers with proven deployments in industrial asset tracking, computer vision security, or fluid quality sensing across BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam, and Indonesia.
  • Commitment to Follow Through: Selected startups must confirm site pilot parameters with field operations teams before installing equipment, participate in initial operational/compliance scoping calls, and fly to Indonesia to instrument test sites and transfer points.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Jakarta, Special Capital Region of Jakarta, Java (Headquarters / Scoping Calls) & Remote Extraction, Storage, and Transfer Sites, Indonesia.
  • Timeline: November 2026 – March 2027
