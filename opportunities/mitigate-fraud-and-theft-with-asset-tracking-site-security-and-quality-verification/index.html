Fraud & Theft Mitigation: Precision Asset Tracking, AI Site Security, and Inline Quality Verification

Thematic Area: Security

Problem Statement: How might we prevent crude oil theft and product adulteration through integrated asset tracking, site monitoring, and continuous quality verification solutions to eliminate undetected inventory losses and secure supply chain integrity across remote extraction and transfer points?

Background

Preventing physical crude oil losses, equipment tampering, and product adulteration during custody transfer requires an integrated monitoring system that combines precision geofenced asset tracking with AI-powered site surveillance and inline fuel quality verification.

The objective is to establish immediate visibility over volume discrepancies, secure remote extraction and storage sites, and reconcile both quantity and quality changes at critical transfer points. Relevant startup capabilities include industrial IoT asset tracking, computer vision security analytics, and continuous inline fluid quality instrumentation.

Assess the solution against the operator’s existing site infrastructure, custody-transfer audit requirements, and remote operational constraints.

Significance of the Problem

  • Inventory Shrinkage & Financial Loss: Severe, unquantified financial losses from stolen crude oil stock, missing equipment, and tampered fuel batches during transport and storage.
  • Site Security & Worker Hazards: Unauthorized site access, intruder incursions, and equipment tampering compromising worker safety, facility operational continuity, and physical infrastructure at remote sites.
  • Supply Chain Degradation: Contractual, legal, and reputational damage when adulterated or off-spec crude oil reaches trading partners due to undetected quality changes during custody transfer.
  • Remote Connectivity Constraints: Deploying continuous site monitoring and continuous quality verification systems in remote extraction areas with low or zero internet connectivity presents technical transmission challenges.
  • Dual-Vector Blindspots: Quality monitoring alone cannot verify physical volume theft unless paired directly with asset tracking and volume reconciliation, while physical site monitoring alone misses deliberate chemical adulteration during active product transfer.

Potential Market Size

  • Midstream Oil Transportation & First-Mile Logistics (Indonesia): Within Indonesia’s USD 14.6 Billion oil and gas market in 2026 (USD 10.72 Billion upstream; USD 2.29 Billion downstream), securing first-mile crude haulage and midstream transit is anchored by the Indonesia Fleet Management Market, generating USD 2.275 Billion in 2026 (13.20% CAGR to USD 4.229 Billion by 2031), driven by the need to prevent tanker diversion and transit volume losses.
  • Tech Security Across Upstream, Midstream & Downstream (Indonesia): Anchored by the Indonesia CCTV Camera Market valued at USD 1.99 Billion (18.35% CAGR to USD 9.05 Billion by 2034) and the Indonesia Cybersecurity Market generating USD 1.62 Billion in 2026 (20.12% CAGR to USD 4.06 Billion by 2031), adoption spans three tiers: Upstream (remote site monitoring and intrusion detection), Midstream (AI video analytics to interpret site and transit recordings), and Downstream (centralized footage review and forensic response).
  • Complementary factors: Mandatory regulatory audits requiring continuous custody-transfer accountability; severe financial pressure to eliminate unaccounted-for crude losses and adulteration; high safety and environmental liabilities from illegal tapping or tanker diversion; and the lack of viable non-automated alternatives across remote terrain.

Qualification & Fitness

  • Technology Readiness: Ready commercial technologies and pilot-proven products exist today for deployment, requiring field testing to confirm performance under remote, low/zero internet conditions.
  • NUS Linkage: Built by research teams at the National University of Singapore (NUS) or supported through the BLOCK71 network, Technology Innovation Programme (TIG), or National GRIP Programme. Open to NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs), professors, as well as local and international technology providers with proven deployments in industrial asset tracking, computer vision security, or fluid quality sensing across BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam, and Indonesia.
  • Commitment to Follow Through: Selected startups must confirm site pilot parameters with field operations teams before installing equipment, participate in initial operational/compliance scoping calls, and fly to Indonesia to instrument test sites and transfer points.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Jakarta, Special Capital Region of Jakarta, Java (Headquarters / Scoping Calls) & Remote Extraction, Storage, and Transfer Sites, Indonesia.
  • Timeline: November 2026 – March 2027
