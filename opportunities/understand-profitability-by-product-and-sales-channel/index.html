Understand Profitability by Product and Sales Channel

Thematic Area: Digital Operations and Automation

How might we calculate reliable product and channel contribution margins to guide pricing, sourcing and operating decisions?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer sells raw ingredients, grocery products and prepared foods through different channels. This project focuses on product and channel costing, with the finance and operating teams defining the priority decisions and establishing the accuracy of current records.

The objective is to connect revenue with the relevant ingredient, purchasing, yield, handling, processing, packaging, delivery, fee and promotion costs. Clear costing enables the business to compare options on their financial effect alongside sales volume.

Relevant startup capabilities include product-cost modelling, finance-data integration and scenario analysis. Agree how contribution margin is defined, which costs are included and which records support a reliable baseline.

Significance of Problem

  • Less informed pricing: Missing costs give the business an incomplete view of product economics when assessing prices and promotions.
  • Unclear channel trade-offs: Inconsistent costing makes it difficult to compare channels with different delivery charges, commissions and discount structures.
  • Overstated improvement claims: Inconsistent treatment of yield, allocated costs and shared benefits inflates reported savings.

Existing Efforts

  • Available modelling capability: An existing cost-management platform organises cost inputs, assumptions, scenario comparisons and approvals. Configure recipes, saleable output, packaging and channel costs to create a retailer-specific food-product margin model.
  • First application: Select one prepared-food product and one channel, agree the costing method and reconcile the model with actual purchasing, production and finance records. Compare a limited pricing, ingredient or packaging scenario using transparent assumptions.
  • Implementation priorities: Establish data ownership, update frequency and the treatment of wastage, returns and shared costs. Assign responsibility for the commercial decision supported by the model and track its realised effect. Use financial scenarios and measured outcomes to guide savings initiatives and decisions on offer expansion.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian grocery-retail scale: Indonesia’s grocery retail sales reached approximately US$106.2 billion in 2025. This figure indicates the scale of the customer industry for product-cost intelligence and provides broader commercial context for costing software.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
