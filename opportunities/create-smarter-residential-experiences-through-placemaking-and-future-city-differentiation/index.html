Creating Smarter Residential Experiences: Placemaking & Future-City Differentiation

Thematic Area: Smart Cities & Real Estate

How might we introduce smart-city technologies and robotics into BSD City to create engaging experiences and make the township more attractive to live, learn, work, and play?

Background

Declining residential housing sales require new attractions and differentiators that position BSD City as Indonesia’s premier futuristic township rather than competing solely on price.

The objective is to establish a distinct placemaking experience layer alongside visible next-generation physical infrastructure. Startups can contribute interactive urban-discovery platforms, building accessibility layers for ground/aerial robots, and AI-driven sustainable urban design modeling.

Smart city experience innovation and placemaking are the main focus. High-impact tech integrations that increase township vibrancy are welcome.

Significance of the Problem

  • Declining residential absorption: Slower housing sales reduce revenue momentum across residential property developments.
  • Lack of township differentiation: Competing purely on property pricing fails to attract tech-savvy homebuyers seeking modern urban living.
  • Infrastructure integration hurdles: Deploying physical robotics or aerial delivery requires building accessibility layers before hardware can operate safely.
  • Fragmented visitor engagement: Without a unified digital discovery layer, township amenities and lifestyle offerings remain underutilized.

Potential Market Size

  • National market scale: The Indonesia residential real estate market is valued at USD 47.99 Billion to USD 70.37 Billion, with residential property accounting for a 54.79% share of the total national real estate sector.
  • Township development scale: BSD City spans an extensive 6,000 to 10,000+ hectares land bank supporting an active footprint of over 500,000 residents and 40,000+ completed housing units, providing a large-scale real-world testing ground for placemaking tech.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG, or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs), and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam, and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: BSD City, Tangerang, Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027
