Assess Solar Trackers for Suitable Project Sites

Thematic Area: Climate and Clean Energy

How might we identify sites where solar tracking can improve lifetime project value after accounting for installation, operating and maintenance requirements?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has named solar tracker systems among the technologies it wants to explore. It seeks innovations that strengthen the value and differentiation of its solar solutions.

The objective is to build the technical and financial case for solar tracking at a suitable project site. Relevant partners provide tracker hardware, control systems, design support or performance modelling.

Tracker selection requires a dedicated project assessment alongside routine rooftop monitoring. Implementation priorities are to select the project type, establish available space and mounting requirements, and define customer requirements. Evaluate the solution for each selected site using technical and commercial suitability criteria.

Significance of Problem

  • Missed suitable applications: A lack of comparison with fixed systems leaves suitable tracking opportunities unexamined.
  • Weak customer economics: Added equipment and maintenance requirements are difficult to justify without a credible lifetime assessment.
  • Integration challenges: Selecting a tracker without site and engineering checks creates layout, structural and operating risks.

Existing Efforts

  • Company-stated need: Solar trackers are explicitly requested. Select a supplier and preferred design using the company’s project requirements and relevant installation evidence.
  • Partner selection: Recruit specialist tracker hardware and engineering partners to complement the available monitoring and project-support tools.
  • Proposed collaboration: Select a technically suitable candidate site and compare fixed-tilt and tracking alternatives using consistent assumptions. Review expected generation, capital cost, shading, control requirements, wind conditions, maintenance access and local support. Obtain engineering approval for the design and financial assumptions. Measure trial operation and output before wider commercial commitments.

Potential Market Size

  • Global tracker demand: Global solar-tracker equipment shipments exceeded 134 GWdc in 2025, increasing 19% from 2024. European shipments reached 25 GWdc. These equipment-shipment figures provide global demand context. Select Indonesian projects using site-specific technical and commercial criteria.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
