Improve Freight Planning with Predictive Transport Management

Thematic Area: Logistic & Shipping

How might we use an AI-powered transport-management system with route prediction to improve freight planning and delivery performance?

Background

Improving freight transport planning requires a transport-management system (TMS) that combines operational records with predictive route analysis to support planning decisions.

The objective is to organise transport work, anticipate route conditions and improve the use of vehicles and delivery resources. Relevant startup capabilities include transport-management software, logistics optimisation, mapping and location analytics.

Assess the solution against the business’s existing systems, shipment requirements and operating constraints.

Significance of the Problem

  • Higher transport costs: Inefficient routes and resource allocation increase travel and operating expenses.
  • Less reliable delivery plans: Limited insight into route performance makes schedules harder to maintain.
  • Slower planning: Teams spend additional time comparing routes, assigning resources and responding to disruptions.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian first-mile operations: Illustrative first-mile spending of US$0.8–1.7 billion for parcels and US$1.5–3.0 billion for freight forwarding follows assumed 10–20% allocations of the respective 2026 markets. These service-spending estimates overlap and should not be added.
  • Operational-efficiency software: Allocating an assumed 45% of the US$9–30 million combined annual software opportunity implies US$4.1–13.5 million annually, with a base case around US$9 million. Applications include pickup scheduling, dispatch, routing, consolidation, capacity planning and driver applications. The combined model assumes 4,000 potential buyers spending US$187.50–625 monthly, with broad adoption.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline
