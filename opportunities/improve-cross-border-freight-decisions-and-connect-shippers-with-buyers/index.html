Improve Cross-Border Freight Decisions and Connect Shippers with Buyers

Thematic Area: Market Access and Business Growth

How might we combine freight-rate predictions, destination-specific trade guidance and shipper–buyer connections to support cross-border business growth?

Background

A shipping and logistics group wants better market intelligence and commercial connections for its freight-forwarding business. It requires an export/import (EXIM) system that predicts freight-rate trends and provides destination-specific guidance on export opportunities, requirements and restrictions.

It also wants a marketplace connecting shippers and buyers. The objective is to support better quotations, help customers assess cross-border opportunities and generate relevant commercial connections.

During project scoping, agree priority trade lanes, commodity categories and the intended buyer type: freight-service customers or buyers of shipped goods.

Significance of Problem

  • Less informed quotations: Limited freight intelligence weakens the team’s ability to account for changing conditions when preparing offers.
  • Slower trade decisions: Customers spend additional time finding reliable destination-specific information.
  • Missed business opportunities: Disconnected shippers and suitable buyers limit commercial growth.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: A Vietnam-focused logistics platform offers warehouse and transport capacity, quotations and cargo tracking for destination arrangements on relevant shipments. Document-processing tools support EXIM paperwork.
  • Implementation priorities: Source freight-rate prediction, country-guidance and shipper–buyer matching capabilities to complete the solution. Maintain destination guidance against current official requirements and test predictions against actual trade-lane data.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian trade activity: Indonesia recorded US$282.91 billion in exports and US$241.86 billion in imports in 2025, increasing 6.15% and 2.83%, respectively, from 2024. These figures indicate the scale of underlying EXIM activity.
  • Freight-forwarding industry: The global services market was estimated at €208.1 billion in 2025, with a forecast of €233.0 billion by 2030. This provides customer-industry context for trade-intelligence tools.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
