Improve Cross-Border Freight Decisions and Connect Shippers with Buyers

Thematic Area: Logistic & Shipping

How might we combine freight-rate predictions, destination-specific trade guidance and shipper–buyer connections to support cross-border business growth?

Background

Cross-border freight operations benefit from stronger market intelligence and commercial connections. This challenge seeks an export/import (EXIM) system that predicts freight-rate trends and provides destination-specific guidance on export opportunities, requirements and restrictions.

It also wants a marketplace connecting shippers and buyers. The objective is to support better quotations, help customers assess cross-border opportunities and generate relevant commercial connections.

During project scoping, agree priority trade lanes, commodity categories and the intended buyer type: freight-service customers or buyers of shipped goods.

Significance of the Problem

  • Less informed quotations: Limited freight intelligence weakens the team’s ability to account for changing conditions when preparing offers.
  • Slower trade decisions: Customers spend additional time finding reliable destination-specific information.
  • Missed business opportunities: Disconnected shippers and suitable buyers limit commercial growth.

Potential Market Size

  • Global freight-forwarding industry: Transport Intelligence estimates the global freight-forwarding services market at €208.1 billion in 2025, growing to €233 billion by 2030. This is the customer industry served by freight intelligence and marketplace solutions.
  • Trade-management technology: Grand View Research estimates the global trade-management software market at US$1.4 billion in 2025, reaching US$3.1 billion by 2033. This covers tools for managing international trade processes, documentation and compliance.
  • Indonesian cross-border trade: Indonesia recorded US$524.77 billion in goods trade in 2025, comprising US$282.91 billion in exports and US$241.86 billion in imports. These underlying trade flows create a substantial base for freight services, trade guidance and buyer connections.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.

  • Digital freight marketplaces already handle substantial business: Freightos facilitated approximately 1.6 million transactions in 2025, up 26%, with US$1.29 billion in gross booking value. Its platform connected buyers with 77 active carriers in the fourth quarter, demonstrating established adoption of online freight comparison and booking. (Source: Freightos FY2025 results (opens in a new tab))
  • Major shippers use freight intelligence in purchasing decisions: Nestlé adopted Xeneta to access current ocean-freight rates and market analysis. The customer study reports simpler contract management and less manual work, showing that reliable rate intelligence has a clear role in day-to-day procurement and commercial decisions. (Source: Xeneta customer story (opens in a new tab))
  • Indonesian exporters are actively seeking overseas buyers: Trade Expo Indonesia 2025 attracted 8,045 international buyers from 130 countries, according to the Ministry of Trade. This participation demonstrates an established need for services that help exporters identify buyers, build commercial relationships and pursue international sales.
