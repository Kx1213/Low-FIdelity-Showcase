Improve Cross-Border Freight Decisions and Connect Shippers with Buyers

Thematic Area: Trade Intelligence & Freight Market Access

How might we combine freight-rate predictions, destination-specific trade guidance and shipper–buyer connections to support cross-border business growth?

Background

A shipping and logistics group wants better market intelligence and commercial connections for its freight-forwarding business. It requires an export/import (EXIM) system that predicts freight-rate trends and provides destination-specific guidance on export opportunities, requirements and restrictions.

It also wants a marketplace connecting shippers and buyers. The objective is to support better quotations, help customers assess cross-border opportunities and generate relevant commercial connections.

The collaboration will define priority trade lanes, commodity categories and buyer profiles, including freight-service customers and buyers of shipped goods.

Significance of Problem

Key challenges include:

  • Less informed quotations: Changing freight conditions make accurate, competitive quotations harder to prepare.
  • Slower trade decisions: Finding current, destination-specific export information adds time to customer decisions.
  • Missed business opportunities: Disconnected shippers and buyers make relevant cross-border opportunities harder to pursue.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: A Vietnam-focused logistics platform offers warehouse and transport capacity, quotations and cargo tracking for relevant shipments. Document-processing tools support EXIM paperwork.
  • Collaboration focus: Bring together freight-rate prediction, destination-specific guidance and shipper–buyer matching. Source the specialist capabilities, maintain current trade guidance and evaluate predictions against actual trade-lane data.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian trade activity: Indonesia recorded US$282.91 billion in exports and US$241.86 billion in imports in 2025, increasing 6.15% and 2.83%, respectively, from 2024. These trade flows provide a substantial operating base for EXIM services.
  • Global freight-forwarding services: The global services market was estimated at €208.1 billion in 2025 and is forecast to reach €233.0 billion by 2030.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
