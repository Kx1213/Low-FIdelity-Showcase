Improve Residential Property Sales Through Better Buyer Targeting and Customer Engagement

Thematic Area: Property

How might we use property and buyer insights to attract qualified prospects, improve customer engagement and convert more enquiries into residential property sales?

Background

To reverse declining residential property sales, a property developer seeks technology partners that can help identify suitable buyers and improve how prospective customers progress from initial interest to completed purchases.

The objective is to strengthen buyer acquisition and sales conversion through better market intelligence, targeted marketing and consistent customer engagement. Relevant startup capabilities include property intelligence, geospatial analytics, customer segmentation, digital marketing, AI-assisted enquiry handling and customer relationship management.

An initial collaboration could focus on one residential development, covering buyer targeting, enquiry qualification, follow-ups and viewing appointments. Define the priority customer segment, available property and customer data, Indonesian-language requirements and compatibility with existing sales systems during project scoping. Agree measurable outcomes such as qualified enquiries, response times, viewing attendance and lead-to-sale conversion.

Significance of the Problem

  • Lower sales revenue: Fewer completed purchases reduce sales receipts and slow the sale of available residential units.
  • Inefficient marketing spending: Campaigns that reach audiences without sufficient interest, purchasing capacity or product fit increase the cost of generating qualified enquiries.
  • Lost buyer interest: Delayed responses and inconsistent follow-ups can cause prospective buyers to disengage before arranging a viewing or making a purchase.
  • Weaker commercial decisions: Limited visibility into buyer behaviour and sales progress makes it harder to identify effective campaigns, prioritise prospects and address conversion bottlenecks.

Potential Market Size

  • Global property-technology market: Grand View Research values the global proptech market at US$45.1 billion in 2025 and projects US$115.0 billion by 2033. This includes technology supporting property operations, transactions and customer engagement.
  • Residential technology segment: Residential applications accounted for 56.3% of global proptech revenue in 2025, making housing the category's largest property segment. The segment includes a broad range of residential technology, beyond sales tools alone.
  • Indonesian housing need: BPS recorded a homeownership backlog of 9.29 million households in 2026. This establishes substantial unmet ownership needs; buyer targeting must match purchasing capacity, location and the homes available.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

  • Developers face immediate sales pressure: Bank Indonesia reported that primary residential property sales fell 8.43% year on year in Q3 2026.
