Track Fresh Inventory from Purchase to Sale or Loss

Thematic Area: Food Innovation and Sustainability

How might we make fresh and frozen inventory movements, markdowns and losses visible by product and outlet to identify where improvements create value?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer sells fresh, frozen and prepared foods through physical and online channels. This project focuses on inventory yield by establishing a clear record of spoilage, markdowns and stock write-offs across a selected part of the business.

The objective is to measure how purchased inventory becomes sales, transfers, markdowns, processing inputs or recorded losses. Use this baseline to quantify the value at stake and select a focused intervention.

Relevant startup capabilities include inventory software, batch and date records, stock-count tools and operational analytics. Pilot planning will document current systems and record availability, then select the product category and outlets for the trial.

Significance of Problem

  • Hidden loss drivers: Unexplained stock adjustments and markdowns obscure where inventory value is being reduced.
  • Harder improvement decisions: Incomplete records prevent effective comparison of purchasing, handling, allocation and preservation options.
  • Unreliable financial measurement: Treating transfers, processing inputs, physical waste and reduced selling prices as the same event overstates savings and obscures their causes.

Existing Efforts

  • Existing operating context: The retailer checks freshness, condition, cleanliness, packaging and dates. Build on these quality controls by mapping how staff record inventory movements and losses.
  • Available capability: Configurable inventory software tracks purchases, receipts, transfers, sales, counts and adjustments across locations. Configure food-specific batch, date, loss-reason and quantity fields to match the retailer’s workflow.
  • First application: Track one category through a small group of outlets and reconcile system movements with physical counts. Establish a baseline by quantity and value, then review the largest measured loss reasons. Measure visibility improvements separately from subsequent interventions and count each benefit once across allocation, preservation and product conversion.

Potential Market Size

  • Scale of global retail food waste: Retail food waste worldwide was estimated at 131 million tonnes in 2022, including edible and associated inedible parts. This figure describes the scale of the global sector challenge; the pilot baseline will quantify the retailer’s own inventory losses.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
