Track Fresh Inventory from Purchase to Sale or Loss

Thematic Area: Fresh-Food Inventory Intelligence & Loss Measurement

How might we make fresh and frozen inventory movements, markdowns and losses visible by product and outlet to identify where improvements would create value?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer sells fresh, frozen and prepared foods through physical and online channels. The opportunity is to make inventory movements and value recovery visible across products and outlets.

The objective is to track how purchased inventory becomes sales, transfers, markdowns, processing inputs or recorded losses. A shared view supports focused decisions on purchasing, handling, allocation and preservation.

Relevant startups offer inventory software, batch and date records, stock-count tools and operational analytics. The collaboration centres on a selected category and a practical stock-recording workflow across participating outlets.

Significance of Problem

The key challenges are:

  • Hidden loss drivers: Unexplained stock adjustments and markdowns make it harder to identify where inventory value is being reduced.
  • Harder improvement decisions: Incomplete records complicate comparisons between purchasing, handling, allocation and preservation options.
  • Inconsistent financial measurement: Transfers, processing inputs, physical waste and reduced selling prices affect inventory value in different ways and require distinct records.

Existing Efforts

  • Existing quality practices: The retailer describes checks on freshness, condition, cleanliness, packaging and dates as part of its product-quality practices.
  • Available capability: Configurable inventory software tracks purchases, receipts, transfers, sales, counts and adjustments across locations. Configure batch, date, loss-reason and quantity fields for food-specific recording.
  • Collaboration focus: Track one category through selected outlets and reconcile system movements with physical counts. Review loss reasons by quantity and value, then prioritise an operating intervention and measure its financial effect.

Potential Market Size

  • Scale of global retail food waste: Global retail food waste was estimated at 131 million tonnes in 2022, including edible and associated inedible parts. This illustrates the scale of the food-waste challenge across the retail sector.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
