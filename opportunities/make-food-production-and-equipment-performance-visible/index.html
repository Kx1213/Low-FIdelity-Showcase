Make Food Production and Equipment Performance Visible

Thematic Area: Digital Operations and Automation

How might we connect production and equipment data to identify practical improvements in the output and consistency of prepared-food production?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer also operates ready-to-eat, ready-to-heat and ready-to-cook product lines. This project focuses on production efficiency, with the production team selecting the priority process and defining success measures during pilot planning.

The objective is to establish a shared view of production steps, material movements, equipment use and output. This gives the operating team a basis for distinguishing capacity constraints from waiting, downtime and process variation before choosing an improvement.

Relevant startup capabilities include manufacturing execution systems, equipment monitoring, industrial data integration and production analytics. Selection criteria include equipment connectivity, available records and compatibility with the food-production environment.

Significance of Problem

  • Unclear improvement priorities: An incomplete operating baseline directs investment toward activities with limited influence on production performance.
  • Harder production planning: Limited visibility of actual output and equipment availability makes realistic production schedules difficult to establish.
  • Unmeasured operating costs: Waiting, stoppages and repeated work consume capacity, while incomplete records obscure their full effect.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: A manufacturing digitalisation provider offers production tracking, industrial IoT and internal material-movement automation. An equipment-monitoring platform consolidates machine, sensor and operating data to analyse performance and downtime. The pilot will assess these capabilities against the retailer’s production requirements.
  • First application: Select one production step and compare recorded input, saleable output, operating time and stoppage reasons with direct observation. Agree who reviews the information and which decisions it supports.
  • Implementation priorities: Establish asset and process fit, the cost of data capture, integration requirements and a measurable improvement target. Connect monitoring results to a defined process change and evaluate its effect on production performance.

Potential Market Size

  • Global production-software market: The manufacturing execution systems market was valued at US$17.6 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach US$41.6 billion by 2033, with 11.7% annual growth during 2026–2033. These figures span manufacturing sectors and provide broader context for prepared-food monitoring.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
