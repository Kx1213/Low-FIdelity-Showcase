Connect Solar Project Spending with Billing and Cash Records

Thematic Area: Digital Operations and Automation

How might we connect project purchases, spending approvals and customer balances to improve visibility of solar project costs and cash requirements?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has identified project management as a priority. This project focuses on cost and cash visibility within project management, with scope selected around the business’s financial workflow.

The objective is to give project and finance teams a usable view of commitments, actual spending, billing and outstanding payments. Relevant startups offer invoice matching, project budgets, spending approvals or receivables management.

Agree the scope with the business before selecting software. Implementation priorities are to map accounting-system connections, project coding and approval rules, and select records for the pilot.

Significance of Problem

  • Unclear project spending: Incomplete links between purchases and project budgets make cost exposure harder to assess.
  • Difficult cash planning: Fragmented records make outstanding customer balances and upcoming commitments harder to compare.
  • More reconciliation work: Invoice discrepancies and inconsistent financial records require additional manual investigation.

Existing Efforts

  • Implementation priorities: Build on the company’s project-management priority by mapping cost control, invoicing and collections. Document existing financial processes and baseline measures, then select the financial workflow to improve.
  • Available partner capability: A finance-automation provider compares supplier invoices with purchase orders and goods receipts. It also offers spending approvals, budget controls, customer-balance tracking, reconciliation and collection reminders. Use these tools to support financial records, with project-margin and cash forecasts built from agreed project and accounting assumptions.
  • Proposed collaboration: Test a selected project’s purchase records and customer balances against the accounting system. Agree how to represent commitments, changes, milestones and actual costs. Evaluate record accuracy, exception resolution and the usefulness of management views. Confirm Indonesian finance and accounting requirements before deployment.

Potential Market Size

  • Solar capital expenditure context: Global investment in solar power projects is estimated at US$365 billion in 2026. This indicates the scale of spending associated with solar delivery and provides sector context for project-finance tools.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
