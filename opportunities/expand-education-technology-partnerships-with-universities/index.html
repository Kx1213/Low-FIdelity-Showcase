Expand Education Technology Partnerships with Universities

Thematic Area: Education and Learning Innovation

How might we identify universities with suitable needs and convert those opportunities into successful institutional partnerships?

Background

An education technology business wants to expand its business-to-business university market. It needs a clearer understanding of which institutions need its products, who makes purchasing decisions and what evidence supports adoption.

The objective is to establish a repeatable approach to identifying institutions, understanding their requirements and progressing from initial engagement to pilot and procurement.

Relevant partner capabilities include audience research, institutional engagement, sales and implementation expertise. Define the priority university segment, current customer base and geographic coverage during project scoping.

Significance of Problem

  • Slower customer growth: Gaps between initial university interest and institutional contracts restrict growth.
  • Longer sales cycles: Unclear purchasing processes and decision-making responsibilities delay agreements.
  • Poor product positioning: Proposals that fail to connect the product with teaching and administrative needs weaken adoption.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: Education technology providers offer learning platforms, teaching support and administrative features for institutional partnership discussions. University marketing research offers audience analysis and content preparation.
  • Implementation priorities: Connect marketing support to a practical institutional sales and procurement process. Select a university segment, establish its purchasing requirements and test a relevant demonstration or pilot. Measure progress through qualified institutional opportunities, procurement milestones and contracts.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesia’s higher education landscape comprised 4,303 institutions and 34,711 study programmes in the 2024/25 academic year, based on a December 2025 data snapshot. Segment this institutional base by product fit, budgets and procurement requirements to define the target market.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
