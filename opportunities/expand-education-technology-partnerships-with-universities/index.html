Expand Education Technology Partnerships with Universities

Thematic Area: Higher Education Market Access & Institutional Growth

How might we identify universities with suitable needs and convert those opportunities into successful institutional partnerships?

Background

An education technology business wants to expand its business-to-business university market. It is seeking institutions whose needs fit its products, the relevant purchasing decision-makers and a clear route from engagement to adoption.

The objective is to establish a repeatable approach to identifying institutions, understanding requirements and progressing from initial engagement to pilot and procurement.

Relevant partners contribute audience research, institutional engagement, sales and implementation expertise. The collaboration will define a priority university segment and geographic focus.

Significance of Problem

Key challenges include:

  • Slower customer growth: Institutional interest delivers limited commercial value without a clear route to contract.
  • Longer sales cycles: Complex purchasing processes and distributed decision-making lengthen institutional sales cycles.
  • Poor product positioning: Proposals must connect product capabilities with an institution’s teaching and administrative priorities.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: Education technology providers offer learning platforms, teaching support and administrative features for partnership discussions. University marketing research offers audience analysis and content preparation.
  • Collaboration focus: Connect marketing support with institutional sales and procurement. Select a university segment, map purchasing requirements and deliver a relevant demonstration or pilot. Track qualified opportunities, procurement progress and contracts.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian higher education landscape: Indonesia’s 2025 Higher Education Statistics records 4,303 higher education institutions and 34,711 study programmes, reflecting the 2024/25 academic year and a December 2025 data snapshot. This extensive institutional landscape supports targeted education-technology partnerships.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
