Expand Education Technology Partnerships with Universities

Thematic Area: Education

How might we identify universities with suitable needs and convert those opportunities into successful institutional partnerships?

Background

Expanding education technology in the university market requires a clearer understanding of institutional needs, purchasing decision-makers and the evidence that supports adoption.

The objective is to establish a repeatable approach to identifying institutions, understanding their requirements and progressing from initial engagement to pilot and procurement.

Relevant partner capabilities include audience research, institutional engagement, sales and implementation expertise. Define the priority university segment, current customer base and geographic coverage during project scoping.

Significance of the Problem

  • Slower customer growth: Gaps between initial university interest and institutional contracts restrict growth.
  • Longer sales cycles: Unclear purchasing processes and decision-making responsibilities delay agreements.
  • Poor product positioning: Proposals that fail to connect the product with teaching and administrative needs weaken adoption.

Potential Market Size

  • Global education-technology market: Grand View Research estimates the global market at US$187.0 billion in 2025, reaching US$437.5 billion by 2033. This covers schools, universities and other learning markets.
  • Higher-education technology: Polaris Market Research values this market at US$54.63 billion in 2025 and forecasts US$262.02 billion by 2034, covering hardware, software and services for higher-education institutions.
  • Indonesian institutional customer base: Indonesia recorded 4,303 higher-education institutions and 34,711 study programmes in its 2025 statistics, providing a substantial base for university partnerships and institutional sales.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.

Market Validation

Expand Education Technology Partnerships with Universities

  • Proven adoption in Indonesia: Universitas Indonesia's partnership with Coursera generated more than 20,000 course enrolments after its launch in 2022. The university integrated industry certificates and online courses into its curriculum, showing how an institutional partnership turns digital content into an established teaching offer. (Source: Coursera (opens in a new tab))
  • Universities already allocate digital budgets: A 2026 TCS survey of 200 senior university leaders in the US, UK and Australia found that institutions allocate 29% of IT budgets to digital student experience and 19% to digital transformation. These existing budget priorities create clear entry points for partners solving teaching, student-service and administrative problems. (Source: TCS (opens in a new tab))
  • Multiple tools are already part of university operations: Instructure's 2026 analysis of nearly 19.5 million US higher-education users found that institutions used an average of 28 integrated learning tools. This demonstrates an established market for specialist products that work with existing university systems and meet institutional requirements. (Source: Instructure (opens in a new tab))
