Establish and Extend Prepared-Food Shelf Life

Thematic Area: Food Innovation and Sustainability

How might we establish the shelf life of a selected prepared-food product and test changes that extend it while maintaining safety and acceptable quality?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates ready-to-eat, ready-to-heat and ready-to-cook product lines. This project focuses on longer shelf life, with the company selecting the product, documenting its current shelf life and setting a target during pilot planning.

The objective is to identify the factors limiting a selected product’s usable life and evaluate suitable changes to packaging, processing or storage. Any longer shelf-life claim must be supported by product-specific evidence under the intended distribution and use conditions.

Relevant partner capabilities include food-preservation technology, packaging expertise, microbiological testing and sensory evaluation. Select the approach using the recipe, preparation process, storage temperature and intended customer use.

Significance of Problem

  • Limited selling flexibility: A short selling period restricts distribution and production-planning options.
  • Product-quality deterioration: Changes without appropriate testing compromise the qualities customers expect throughout the claimed shelf life.
  • Unsupported commercial decisions: Extending a product’s shelf life without product-specific safety and quality evidence exposes the business to unsafe or unsuitable release decisions.

Existing Efforts

  • Existing starting point: The retailer has an operating prepared-food line. Review its preservation process, shelf-life testing records and measured reasons for end of life to guide technology selection.
  • Available capabilities: A biodegradable-film provider offers an alternative packaging material. Pair material assessment with specialist preservation expertise and product-specific testing to establish performance for the selected food.
  • First application: Choose one product, establish the current baseline and agree a competent testing plan. Compare a defined change with the existing process using relevant safety, quality and pack-integrity measures. The operating and quality teams must set release criteria, intended conditions, trial duration and the evidence required for any revised shelf-life claim.

Potential Market Size

  • Global food-testing demand: The global food safety testing market is projected to reach US$48.01 billion by 2033, with 7.8% annual growth during 2026–2033. This broader market includes several testing categories and provides adjacent context for product shelf-life testing.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
