Establish and Extend Prepared-Food Shelf Life

Thematic Area: Food Preservation & Product Validation

How might we establish the shelf life of a selected prepared-food product and test changes that extend it while maintaining safety and acceptable quality?

Background

An Indonesian healthy-food retailer operates ready-to-eat, ready-to-heat and ready-to-cook product lines. The opportunity is to extend the usable life of selected products while maintaining safety and acceptable quality.

The objective is to identify what limits a product’s shelf life and compare targeted changes to packaging, processing or storage under its intended distribution and use conditions.

Relevant partners offer food-preservation technology, packaging expertise, microbiological testing and sensory evaluation. The collaboration brings these capabilities together around a selected recipe, preparation process and storage temperature.

Significance of Problem

The key challenges are:

  • Limited selling flexibility: A short selling period restricts distribution options and production-planning flexibility.
  • Quality deterioration: Changes in flavour, appearance, texture and product condition reduce customer acceptance over time.
  • Difficult product decisions: A clear view of safety, quality and pack performance is essential for selecting a shelf-life intervention and setting a suitable selling period.

Existing Efforts

  • Existing prepared-food operations: The retailer already operates prepared-food lines. These provide the product and process context for a focused preservation project.
  • Available packaging capability: A biodegradable-film developer offers an alternative packaging material. The collaboration brings together food-preservation, packaging and testing expertise for product-specific development.
  • Collaboration focus: Choose one product and compare a defined change with its existing process. Measure safety, sensory quality and pack integrity over time. Set the intended storage conditions, trial duration, product-release criteria and target selling period with the operating and quality teams.

Potential Market Size

  • Global food-testing demand: The global food safety testing market is projected to reach US$48.01 billion by 2033, with 7.8% annual growth during 2026–2033. The market covers multiple testing categories supporting food safety and product quality.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
