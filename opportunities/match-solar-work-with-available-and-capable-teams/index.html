Match Solar Work with Available and Capable Teams

Thematic Area: Solar Workforce Planning & Technician Development

How might we match installation and maintenance commitments with workforce availability and demonstrated job capabilities?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has named workforce and HR as an area to address. The opportunity is to connect project and maintenance requirements with team availability and job capabilities.

The objective is to support workforce planning and practical technician development. Relevant startups offer shift planning, competency management, mobile learning and training records.

The solution should reflect work locations, contractor arrangements and role requirements, giving managers useful information for deployment and skills development.

Significance of Problem

Without an effective solution, the challenges include:

  • Difficult resource planning: Project and service commitments are harder to fulfil when availability and team requirements are disconnected.
  • Unclear skills gaps: Agreed role requirements and assessments help managers target the support technicians need.
  • Fragmented procedure access: Scattered instructions and learning records make approved working methods harder to communicate and review.

Existing Efforts

  • Business priority: Workforce and HR is one of the company’s identified needs.
  • Available partner capabilities: An Indonesian workforce offering supports rosters, attendance, timesheets and labour-cost visibility. A competency platform compares assessed capabilities with role requirements. Mobile-learning tools turn approved procedures into lessons, quizzes and job aids with completion records.
  • Collaboration focus: Test a technician roster, assess a selected role or trial an approved maintenance lesson. Combine supervisor assessment of practical capability with attendance and learning records. Include language, travel and contractor arrangements in the workflow.

Potential Market Size

  • Solar workforce scale: Solar PV employed 7.2 million people globally in 2024, with Asia hosting 75% of those jobs. This represents a large workforce for planning and skills-development services.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum technology readiness level 3 (TRL3).
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.
