Match Solar Work with Available and Capable Teams

Thematic Area: Digital Operations and Automation

How might we match installation and maintenance commitments with workforce availability and demonstrated job capabilities?

Background

An Indonesian solar EPC business has named workforce or HR as a problem to address. The project will prioritise a specific need across hiring, retention, technician scheduling or training.

The objective is to define workforce support within the collaboration scope and select a measurable operating need. Relevant startups offer shift planning, competency management, mobile learning or practical training records.

The solution must fit the company’s roles, work locations and contractor arrangements. Select the scope with operating managers and match scheduling software or training content to the chosen workforce need.

Significance of Problem

  • Difficult resource planning: Gaps in workforce-availability records make project and service commitments harder to match with the right teams.
  • Unclear skills gaps: A lack of agreed role requirements and assessments makes it difficult to identify the support technicians need.
  • Inconsistent procedure access: Fragmented instructions and learning records make approved working methods harder to communicate and review.

Existing Efforts

  • Company-stated concern: Workforce or HR is a stated topic. Document the current process and business impact, then select the operating need before choosing a tool.
  • Available partner capabilities: An Indonesian workforce offering supports rosters, attendance, timesheets and labour-cost visibility. A competency platform compares assessed capabilities with role requirements. Mobile-learning tools turn approved procedures into lessons, quizzes and job aids with completion records.
  • Proposed collaboration: Choose one actual constraint: test a technician roster, assess one role against agreed requirements, or trial one approved maintenance lesson. Review practical readiness with supervisors alongside attendance or learning records. Confirm language, travel and contractor needs. Course completion alone does not demonstrate competence to perform technical work.

Potential Market Size

  • Solar workforce scale: Solar PV employed 7.2 million people globally in 2024, with Asia hosting 75% of those jobs. These figures show the scale of the industry workforce served by workforce-planning and technician-development tools.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
