Environmental Remediation: Contaminant Detection, Wastewater Treatment, and Byproduct Capture

Thematic Area: Environmental Remediation & Resource Recovery

Problem Statement: How might we detect, treat, and capture industrial contaminants across produced water and oily waste streams through targeted sensor, membrane, and sorbent technologies to eliminate environmental discharge hazards and reduce freshwater demand in upstream operations?

Background

To address oily wastewater, hard-to-treat persistent organic pollutants, chlorinated compounds, heavy metals, and sludge generated during upstream extraction before environmental discharge occurs.

The goal is to establish the specific dominant contaminant profile (organic pollutants, heavy metals, chlorinated compounds, or oil) to narrow down and deploy targeted detection, treatment, and byproduct management technologies. The objective is to enable early warning of pollution events, purify high-strength industrial wastewater for safe discharge or reuse, and capture residual oils and metals using sustainable sorbents.

Who: Environmental compliance teams, site engineers, and waste management leads. What: Soil/water oil spills, persistent organic pollutants, chlorinated contaminants, heavy metal sludge, and high-strength industrial wastewater. Where: Produced-water discharge points, oily wastewater treatment units, and soil/sludge containment areas across extraction sites. When: Continuously during active production, post-extraction wastewater processing, and site remediation operations. Why: Unfiltered industrial wastewater and unmanaged contaminant leaks lead to environmental non-compliance, regulatory liability, local ecosystem degradation, and excessive freshwater consumption.

By deploying early-detection sensors, advanced oxidation, membrane bioreactors, and specialized sorbents/aerogels to isolate, detoxify, and recover valuable hydrocarbon or metal byproducts. Combines commercial field waste management requirements with a deep portfolio of 9 pre-commercial environmental technologies developed by NUS research teams (including NERI and CDE).

Significance of the Problem

  • Ecosystem Damage: Severe environmental damage to surrounding ecosystems from unmonitored oil leaks or toxic wastewater discharge.
  • Regulatory & License Liabilities: Heavy regulatory penalties and potential suspension of operating licenses due to non-compliance with environmental discharge standards.
  • High Operational Expenditure: High operational costs associated with continuous freshwater procurement and off-site hazardous waste disposal.
  • Mismatched Treatment Selection: Selecting the wrong treatment technology before confirming the exact dominant contaminant type (organics vs. heavy metals vs. chlorinated compounds) leads to failed remediation and wasted capital.
  • TRL Integration Gaps: Varying technological readiness levels across different remediation stages (detection, treatment, byproduct capture) require careful matching to site readiness.

Potential Market Size

  • Midstream Oil Transportation & First-Mile Logistics (Indonesia): Within Indonesia’s USD 14.6 Billion oil and gas market, first-mile oily sludge haulage and midstream waste transit are anchored by the Indonesia Waste Management Market at USD 2.30 Billion (7.80% CAGR to USD 3.61 Billion by 2031) and the Indonesia Fleet Management Market at USD 464 Million (5.5% CAGR to USD 613 Million by 2032).
  • Contaminant Detection, Capture & Treatment (Indonesia): Anchored by the Indonesia Industrial Wastewater Treatment Market at USD 1.61 Billion (7.20% CAGR to USD 2.62 Billion by 2032), deployment covers Upstream (inline contaminant detection), Midstream (sorbent hydrocarbon/metal capture), and Downstream (membrane treatment for discharge or reuse).
  • Complementary factors: Mandatory national wastewater discharge limits; severe legal liabilities from toxic spills; rising produced water in mature fields requiring freshwater recycling; and strict bans on conventional open-pit disposal.

Qualification & Fitness

  • Technology Readiness: Pre-commercial research across different stages of maturity (Electrochemical Regeneration of Activated Carbon and Microbial Remediation of Chlorinated Pollutants are the furthest along; others require early-stage validation).
  • NUS Linkage: 9 specialized technologies developed across NUS research faculties (NERI, CDE, CEE, MSE) and facilitated via BLOCK71 / NUS Enterprise, the Technology Innovation Programme (TIG), or National GRIP Programme. Open to NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs), professors, deep-tech research spin-offs, environmental engineering teams, and startups focused on industrial water treatment and hazardous waste recovery across BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam, and Indonesia.
  • Commitment to Follow Through: Selected teams must participate in technical scoping calls to analyze site-specific contaminant samples, attend technical discussions with NUS Enterprise, and travel to Indonesia for on-site pilot testing.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Jakarta, Special Capital Region of Jakarta, Java (Headquarters / Scoping Calls) & Active Extraction & Wastewater Treatment Sites, Indonesia.
  • Timeline: November 2026 – March 2027
