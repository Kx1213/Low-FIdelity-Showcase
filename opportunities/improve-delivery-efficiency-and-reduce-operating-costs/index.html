Improve Delivery Efficiency and Reduce Operating Costs

Thematic Area: Smart Logistics and Asset Management

How might we identify and improve the delivery activities that create the greatest avoidable costs while maintaining reliable service?

Background

A delivery business wants to improve operational efficiency and reduce costs. It is seeking technology partners that help identify practical improvements across its delivery operations.

The objective is to select a measurable operating problem, test a suitable solution and measure its impact on performance. Applications to assess include route planning, service coverage and hub placement.

Define the workflow, operating constraints and success measures during project scoping. Relevant startup capabilities include logistics software, geospatial analysis, routing and operational analytics.

Significance of Problem

  • Higher delivery costs: Avoidable travel, waiting time and poor coordination increase operating expenses.
  • Limited productivity: Inefficient operations constrain the number of deliveries completed with existing resources.
  • Slower expansion: Unclear operating bottlenecks complicate growth and service reliability.

Existing Efforts

  • Available capabilities: A geospatial platform provides location, traffic, route and heatmap analysis for examining delivery coverage, route conditions and hub locations. A university routing prototype has been tested in eldercare transport; adapt and test it for freight operations as part of partner evaluation.
  • Implementation priorities: Select an operating bottleneck, document existing systems and previous improvement attempts, and establish current performance. Run a focused pilot comparing cost per delivery, travel time, lateness and resource utilisation.

Potential Market Size

  • The global route optimisation software market is estimated at US$12.2 billion in 2026, rising to US$21.5 billion by 2030. This indicates commercial demand for routing technology across transport applications, including delivery operations.

Eligibility / Qualification

  • Technology readiness: Minimum TRL3; suitability for a pilot assessed during selection.
  • NUS linkage: NUS student or graduate founder, or incubation through an NUS programme such as BLOCK71.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to Indonesian and overseas startups.
  • Commitment: Complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Where & When

  • Where: Project location set during pilot planning.
  • When: Schedule set during partner selection.
