Extraction Optimisation: Enhancing Upstream Crude Oil Production and Efficiency

Thematic Area: Energy, Oil and Gas, Sustainability

How might we optimize upstream crude oil extraction and fluid processing to maximize production output while reducing process time, operational expenses, and environmental risks?

Background

From initial exploration and drilling to extraction, fluid separation, and treatment, upstream oil production carries high financial and operational risks. Precise process diagnosis ensures capital is directed precisely where bottlenecks occur, boosting crude yield while cutting downtime and expenses.

The goal is to establish a systematic diagnostic framework that pinpoints exact operational bottlenecks across the entire upstream lifecycle—from subsurface exploration and drilling to extraction, fluid separation, and final treatment. The objective is to maximize overall crude oil yield, accelerate separation cycles, minimize processing downtime, and lower capital expenditure and operational risks across every stage of production.

The work involves exploration leads, drilling engineers, production managers and site operations teams across exploration targets, drilling sites, reservoir wellbores, surface separation units and fluid treatment facilities. Assessment spans initial site evaluation and ongoing extraction, fluid handling and processing.

The approach deploys advanced diagnostic tools (subsurface multiphase flow assessments) alongside high-efficiency separation and treatment technologies (chemical-resistant membranes and targeted sorbents). This combines commercial upstream extraction, drilling, and processing operational expertise with pre-commercial deep-tech engineering, subsurface diagnostics, and advanced membrane research developed by NUS research teams.

Significance of the Problem

  • Exploration & Drilling Risk: High capital misallocation and severe financial losses caused by committing drilling resources without precise subsurface diagnosis of reservoir bottlenecks.
  • Extraction & Yield Loss: Substantial revenue loss from unrecovered hydrocarbons left behind in produced water streams and unoptimized extraction flows.
  • Separation & Operational Expenses: Excessive energy consumption, prolonged processing times, and high operational costs in surface separation units handling complex oil/water/chemical emulsions.
  • Treatment & Environmental Hazards: Suboptimal treatment of produced water and oily waste leading to high freshwater consumption, heavy chemical usage, and risk of environmental non-compliance.
  • Harsh Chemical & Physical Conditions: High-temperature, high-pressure, and chemically harsh environments degrade standard surface filtration equipment and membranes rapidly during separation and treatment.
  • Dynamic Subsurface Complexity: Multiphase fluid interactions deep within the wellbore or reservoir are difficult to analyze dynamically without intrusively stopping ongoing extraction operations.
  • Multi-Stage Bottleneck Isolation: Pinpoint accuracy is required to determine whether an operational bottleneck originates in reservoir permeability, wellbore mechanics, or surface-processing capacity before committing capital.

Potential Market Size

  • Market size and growth: Global annual upstream capital expenditure is valued between USD 500 Billion – USD 600+ Billion, with the global enhanced oil recovery (EOR) and produced water treatment segment representing a market size exceeding USD 50 Billion to USD 57.9 Billion. Growth across exploration, drilling, extraction, separation, and treatment is driven by aging mature fields, natural field decline rates, and the critical need to improve overall process efficiency.
  • Supporting factors: Mandatory compliance with strict international and national environmental discharge limits for produced water, corporate decarbonization and energy-efficiency targets across processing facilities, reducing capital expenditure risks during exploratory drilling.

Qualification & Fitness

  • Technology Readiness: Technologies have demonstrated proof-of-concept in laboratory settings (TRL 4–6) but require field validation against specific crude oil and chemical compositions.
  • NUS Linkage: Built by research teams at the National University of Singapore (NUS) and facilitated through BLOCK71 / NUS Enterprise, PIER71, Technology Innovation Programme (TIG), or the National GRIP programme. Open to NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs), professors, as well as global and local deep-tech ventures and research spin-offs specializing in membrane science or subsurface analytics across BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam, and Indonesia.
  • Commitment to Follow Through: Selected teams must commit to participating in joint technical scoping calls, conducting site data reviews, and traveling to Indonesia for site visits and pilot deployment testing.
