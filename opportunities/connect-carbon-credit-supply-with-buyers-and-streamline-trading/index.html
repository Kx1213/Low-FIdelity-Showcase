Connect Carbon-Credit Supply with Buyers and Streamline Trading

Thematic Area: Energy & Renewable Energy / Climate & Sustainability

How might we connect eligible carbon-credit supply with suitable buyers and manage purchases, sales and transfers more efficiently?

Background

Building carbon-credit buying, selling and intermediation capabilities requires access to suitable counterparties, experienced trading partners and a consistent process for progressing opportunities into completed transactions.

The objective is to build a repeatable commercial process covering buyer qualification, credit-offer comparison, quotations, contracts and transaction tracking. Relevant startup capabilities include carbon marketplaces, business-to-business sales software, transaction workflows and secure document sharing.

Significance of the Problem

  • Missed commercial opportunities: Disconnected buyers and credit suppliers limit transaction opportunities.
  • Slower transactions: Repeated document requests, unclear responsibilities and fragmented approvals delay agreements.
  • Greater execution risk: Incomplete records of credit status, ownership and transfer responsibilities complicate transaction execution.

Potential Market Size

  • Global carbon-credit market: MSCI estimates the primary global carbon-credit market at US$1.4 billion in 2025 and projects it to reach US$5–20 billion by 2030.
  • Growing forward-purchase market: Announced agreements to purchase future carbon credits reached US$12.3 billion in 2025, nearly three times the previous year. These contracts demonstrate substantial commitments to securing future credit supply.
  • Active Indonesian trading market: IDXCarbon recorded Rp36.37 billion in transactions covering 903,915 tonnes of carbon credits in 2025, establishing a local market for buying and selling.

These figures measure credit and contract values, not brokerage or platform revenue, and should not be added together.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

Market Validation

Connect Carbon-Credit Supply with Buyers and Streamline Trading

  • Established end-buyer demand: Buyers retired 202 million carbon credits in 2025, up 3% from the previous year. This demonstrates demand for credits used toward climate commitments and compliance obligations. (Source: MSCI (opens in a new tab))
  • Major companies are securing long-term supply: Microsoft signed agreements for 45 million tonnes of carbon removal with 21 companies in its 2025 financial year, twice the previous year’s contracted volume. These future-delivery agreements demonstrate large-scale corporate demand and the importance of finding reliable suppliers. (Source: Microsoft (opens in a new tab))
  • Trading platforms already operate at scale: Xpansiv’s CBL reports 300 million tonnes of carbon traded since 2020, including exchange transactions and privately negotiated trades settled through its platform. Participation from more than 200 active organisations demonstrates an established role for platforms connecting buyers and sellers. (Source: Xpansiv (opens in a new tab))
