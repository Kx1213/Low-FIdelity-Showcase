Middleman Role Between Carbon Credit Suppliers and Buyers

Thematic Area: Energy & Renewable Energy / Climate & Sustainability

Problem Statement: How might we establish an efficient carbon credit trading middleman business that facilitates transactions between carbon credit suppliers and buyers, while streamlining negotiations, agreements and credit transfers?

Background

Establishing a carbon credit trading intermediary business requires the ability to source eligible carbon credits from suppliers, identify suitable buyers, negotiate commercial terms and facilitate transactions between both parties.

The objective is to develop a scalable and repeatable business model that acts as a middleman in carbon credit transactions, generating revenue through commissions or trading margins. This includes identifying supply and demand opportunities, evaluating credit offerings, coordinating pricing and negotiations, managing contracts and facilitating credit ownership transfers. Relevant startup capabilities include carbon trading platforms, brokerage solutions, transaction management systems and secure document exchange.

Significance of the Problem

  • Limited trading opportunities: Difficulty identifying reliable carbon credit suppliers and interested buyers restricts the ability to facilitate transactions and generate revenue.
  • Inefficient transaction processes: Manual negotiations, fragmented communications and complex documentation slow down transactions between suppliers and buyers.
  • Greater execution risk: Inconsistent verification of carbon credit eligibility, ownership and transfer requirements increases transaction risks and complicates the intermediary's role.
  • Challenges in scaling operations: Reliance on manual sourcing, matchmaking and transaction coordination limits the ability to handle larger trading volumes and expand intermediary services.

Potential Market Size

  • Indonesian carbon industry market size: The combined annual opportunity across carbon projects, MRV services and carbon credit trading could reach approximately US$1.61–1.79 billion by 2030, based on preliminary IDX projections and market assumptions. This includes some overlapping transaction value.
  • Indonesian intermediary opportunity: Brokerage fees could represent approximately US$38–75 million annually by 2030, assuming 50% of US$1.5 billion in annual credit value is sold through brokers charging 5–10% commission. Credit value follows the preliminary IDX scenario; broker participation is an assumption, while commissions reflect a general industry benchmark. This estimates potential fees before operating costs.

Qualification & Fitness

  • NUS founders and programme support: Have an NUS student or graduate founder, or have received incubation support through an NUS programme such as B71, PIER71, TIG or the National GRIP Programme.
  • NUS researchers and academics: NUS researchers, principal investigators (PIs) and professors.
  • Local and overseas startups: Open to startups from Indonesia and other countries.
  • BLOCK71 network: Startups from BLOCK71 centres in Singapore, the United States, Japan, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.
  • Commitment: Be ready to complete the selection process and travel to Indonesia if selected.

Location & Timeline

  • Location: Indonesia
  • Timeline: November 2026–April 2027.
